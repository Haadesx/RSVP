# RSVP and Reading Perception

*Research dossier, compiled 2026-08-29. Composed from four independent research passes plus verification fetches. Primary sources linked inline; anything not retrieved as a primary is marked SECONDHAND or UNVERIFIED.*

## Bottom line

1. **The mechanism RSVP removes is the cheapest one.** Saccades are ~20–35 ms against ~250 ms fixations — about **10% of reading time**, not the 80% Spritz marketed ([Rayner et al. 2016, PSPI](http://faculty.cas.usf.edu/eschotter/papers/Rayner_Schotter_Masson_Potter_Treiman_2016_PSPI.pdf)).
2. **The expensive removals are parafoveal preview, skipping/refixation, and regressions.** Denying preview alone costs 22.5% of reading rate and +27 ms per fixation ([Choi et al. 2015](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/s13414-015-0942-1.pdf)); denying regressions alone drops comprehension 84% → 71–56% ([Schotter, Tran & Rayner 2014](http://faculty.cas.usf.edu/eschotter/papers/Schotter_Tran_Rayner_2014_PsychSci.pdf)).
3. **RSVP is comprehension-neutral at or below normal rate and degrades above it.** Costs appear at ~230–305 *delivered* wpm ([Di Nocera et al. 2018](https://iris.uniroma1.it/retrieve/e3835320-f382-15e8-e053-a505fe0a3de9/Di-Nocera_Rapid_2018.pdf)); page reading with eyes intact holds to ~350–405 wpm ([Schwalm, Radach & Kuperman 2026](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/10888438.2025.2612649)) — **now verified from the CC-BY PDF** (N = 46, speeds 225/270/350/360/405 wpm, comprehension .56/.58/.56/.58/.57/.49), *with the caveat that the instrument is 6 four-option items per narrative passage at 25% chance, so the “hold” is a hold at ~56% absolute.*
4. **What breaks first is literal/verbatim detail, not gist.** Three labs converge: literal 60% vs 72% at parity speed ([Benedetto et al. 2015](https://www.tsw.it/wp-content/uploads/Rapid-serial-visual-presentation-in-reading-The-case-of-Spritz-1.pdf)); detail 82% → 53% at 500 wpm ([Boo & Conklin 2015](https://pops.lancashire.ac.uk/index.php/jsltr/article/view/334/137)). **This is the exact failure mode for a numeric-fidelity reader.**
5. **The ORP/pivot heuristic has one controlled test and it is null on four outcomes (ω² = 0.00).** Its origin is uncited marketing copy plus a patent whose own text credits Brysbaert & Nazir for only four word lengths (3, 5, 7, 9) and admits the rest of its table was interpolated and extrapolated by the inventors to fit a 0.20–0.33 ratio ([Kosch et al. CHI 2020](https://thomaskosch.com/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/kosch2020one.pdf); [US 8,903,174 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8903174B2/en)).
6. **Fixed-interval timing is wrong but variable timing is unproven.** The eye's per-word budget varies ~1.6× on length × predictability alone ([Rayner et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3543826/)); the only direct test of a variable-dwell scheme found preference gain, **no comprehension gain, and a speed loss** ([Castelhano & Muter 2001](https://web.archive.org/web/20071211155000/http://www.psych.utoronto.ca:80/users/muter/castelhanoandmuter.htm)).
7. **Slack belongs between sentences, not inside them.** Slowing the within-sentence rate re-invites eye movements and made efficiency worse ([Rahman & Muter 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10354807/)).
8. **Zero evidence exists on RSVP with numerals, equations, tables or citations.** Three independent search passes found nothing. Ship conservative defaults there and say so.
9. **The strongest pro-RSVP result in the literature is 34 years old and is about decoding, not comprehension** — [Rubin & Turano 1992](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1604858/) got 1171 wpm RSVP vs 303 wpm page (oral reading, 13 observers), and 6 of 13 subjects hit the apparatus ceiling of 1652 wpm at ≥75% on a multiple-choice test. It is the single result an RSVP advocate should be made to confront, and its conditions are why it does not generalise (see §3).
10. **Two silent-failure mechanisms are specific to RSVP timing, not to speed generally:** repetition blindness (~24–30% of repeated words dropped at 117 ms/word, sentences reported ungrammatically without the reader noticing — [Bond & Andrews 2008](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/MC.36.2.295.pdf)) and the attentional blink (a ~270 ms window opening ~180 ms after an attended item, **abolished by inserting a blank interval after it** — [Raymond, Shapiro & Arnell 1992](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.18.3.849)). Both were missing from the first draft of this dossier.

## 1. What normal reading does

Skilled adult silent reading of easy English prose runs on fixations of **200–250 ms (mean 225 ms)** and forward saccades of **7–9 letter spaces (~2° at 4 chars/degree)**, with saccade durations of **20–35 ms** and peak velocities near 500°/s ([Rayner 1998, *Psych. Bulletin*](http://andrewd.ces.clemson.edu/courses/cpsc881/papers/reading/Ray98_readingSurvey.pdf); restated in [Rayner et al. 2016](http://faculty.cas.usf.edu/eschotter/papers/Rayner_Schotter_Masson_Potter_Treiman_2016_PSPI.pdf)). Letter spaces, not degrees, are the invariant — saccade size in letters is stable across viewing distance, which means **RSVP font size does not change the underlying unit**.

The reference per-reader table in both canonical reviews (mean **216 ms, 8.1 chars, 11% regressions, 308 wpm**, n = 10) is [Rayner 1978 data reprinted unchanged](http://andrewd.ces.clemson.edu/courses/cpsc881/papers/reading/Ray98_readingSurvey.pdf) — **~48 years old**, pre-video-eyetracker, still circulating as the 2016 reference. Between-reader spread is wide (190–255 ms; 6.7–9.5 chars; 1–20% regressions; 230–382 wpm). Modern high-rate corpora agree on per-word durations: [GECO](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/s13428-016-0734-0.pdf) (14 English monolinguals, whole Agatha Christie novel, 54,364 tokens, EyeLink 1000 @ 1 kHz, 14 pt Courier New, 3 chars/degree) gives first-fixation **M = 214 ms, SD = 70, range 101–502**; gaze duration 232 ms (SD 89, range 101–695); total reading time 264 ms (SD 127, range 101–1060).

**The distribution, not the mean, is the design-relevant fact.** Within a single passage one reader's fixations span **<100 ms to >500 ms** and saccades **1 to >15 letter spaces** ([Rayner 1998](http://andrewd.ces.clemson.edu/courses/cpsc881/papers/reading/Ray98_readingSurvey.pdf)); GECO's measures were positively skewed and non-normal even after log transform (and its trimming — fixations <100 ms excluded, >2.5 SD removed — *inflates* the lower tail). A uniform dwell replaces a heavy-tailed, cognitively-gated distribution with a constant.

| Mechanism | Value | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Fixation duration | 225 ms mean; 200–250 typical; <100–>500 within-reader | Silent reading, easy English prose, skilled adults |
| Forward saccade | 7–9 letter spaces (~2°); 20–35 ms | Same; 4 chars/degree |
| Regressions | 10–15% of saccades | Rises with difficulty; two populations (see below) |
| Perceptual span | 3–4 letters left, **14–15 right** | Moving-window; a *maximum*, not per-fixation |
| Word-identification span | **7–8 letters right only** | The outer band carries layout, not identity |
| Parafoveal preview benefit | **30–40 ms/word** (80 ms across morpheme boundaries) | Boundary paradigm, per previewed word |
| Word skipping | 30–38% of tokens | Content words fixated ~85%, function words ~35%, "the" ≥50% skipped |
| Reading rate | 200–400 wpm | College-educated good readers |

The span asymmetry is **attentional/linguistic, not acuity-driven**: it reverses in Hebrew and Arabic, flips direction during regressions, and does not grow when parafoveal letters are magnified to equalize acuity ([Rayner et al. 2016](http://faculty.cas.usf.edu/eschotter/papers/Rayner_Schotter_Masson_Potter_Treiman_2016_PSPI.pdf)). It shrinks with text difficulty. The **split between the two spans is the load-bearing structure**: a small window (fixated word + 1–2 right) identifies words; a larger window perceives layout — where words and spaces are — *in order to plan eye movements* ([Schotter, Angele & Rayner 2012](http://faculty.cas.usf.edu/eschotter/papers/Schotter_Angele_Rayner_2012_APP.pdf)).

Disappearing-text experiments localize where the 250 ms goes: a word erased after **60 ms** of direct fixation leaves reading unaffected, and the eyes stay on the now-absent word as long as they would have — but if the word to the *right* disappears after 60 ms, reading is disrupted ([Rayner et al. 2016](http://faculty.cas.usf.edu/eschotter/papers/Rayner_Schotter_Masson_Potter_Treiman_2016_PSPI.pdf)). **The foveal word needs ~60 ms of photons but ~250 ms of clock, and word n+1 must be visible during that window.**

Regressions split into two functionally distinct populations: short oculomotor corrections, and **long (>10 letter spaces) comprehension repairs** aimed accurately at the source of difficulty ([Rayner 1998](http://andrewd.ces.clemson.edu/courses/cpsc881/papers/reading/Ray98_readingSurvey.pdf)). Only the second carries information, and it requires a **spatial memory of where the failure was**.

**Re-reading as a share of time is a derived figure**, not a quoted one — the literature reports regressions as a proportion of *saccades*. From [GECO](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/s13428-016-0734-0.pdf) means: (264−232)/264 = **12.1%** of per-word fixation time is second-pass; go-past (298) vs gaze (232) puts regressive excursions at **22.1%** of go-past time. Cross-checked against [Rayner, Slattery & Bélanger 2010](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/PBR.17.6.834.pdf) Table 1 (13.6% of fixations regressive in fast readers, 19.0% in slow). **Treat 12–22% as a derived band.**

### Conflicts

The 14–15 character span asymptote **does not replicate cleanly**. [Choi et al. 2015](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/s13414-015-0942-1.pdf) (N = 70) found no-window reading still faster than a fixed **16-character** window (244 vs 235 wpm), "inconsistent with the notion that... no additional benefit is obtained beyond the 15-character window", and note Veldre & Andrews (2014) found the same. [Rayner, Slattery & Bélanger](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/PBR.17.6.834.pdf) hit the wall from the other side and explained it via word-length variance in a 3-word window — an explanation that does not cover Choi's fixed-character window. **Trust the direction of the anomaly over either explanation: the standard span figure is a floor, so RSVP's information deficit is if anything understated by it.**

## 2. What RSVP removes, and what each mechanism was for

| Removed | Cost of removal | Function it served |
|---|---|---|
| **The saccade** | ~25 ms/movement, **~10% of clock time** | Bring next region to fovea. *The only thing RSVP targets, and the smallest term.* |
| **Parafoveal preview (n+1)** | **30–40 ms/word**; whole-system: **−22.5% rate, +27 ms/fixation** | Pre-activate abstract letter identity + phonology before fixation |
| **Word skipping** | 30–38% of tokens charged full dwell | Spend zero time on "the" so time flows to hard words |
| **Refixation on long words** | Long words get one look instead of 1.2 | Serial coverage of items longer than the ~7–8-char identification span — *exactly the regime of technical terms and numeric strings* |
| **Regressions** | 10–15% of saccades; 12–22% of time (derived) | Comprehension repair with an accurate spatial target |
| **Cognitively gated fixation duration** | Closed loop → open loop | The reader, not the device, decides when the next word appears |

Two independent moving-window datasets bracket the preview cost. [Choi, Lowder, Ferreira & Henderson 2015](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/s13414-015-0942-1.pdf) (N = 70, EyeLink 1000 @ 1 kHz, 80 *USA Today* paragraphs of 42–61 words, 16 pt Courier New, 2.98 chars/degree, left window fixed at 4): no-window 244 wpm / 211 ms; 4-char right window **189 wpm / 238 ms**. [Rayner, Slattery & Bélanger 2010](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/PBR.17.6.834.pdf) (N = 32, 160 sentences ~11.1 words): no-window 205 ms / 8.9 forward + 1.4 regressive fixations; one-word window **231 ms / 11.3 forward + 2.6 regressive** — +26 ms per fixation, +2.4 forward fixations and +1.2 regressions per 11-word sentence.

Span size is a **real individual difference tracking reading speed**: fast readers (337 wpm) kept improving as window size grew to no-window, while slow readers (207 wpm) hit asymptote at a 2-word window, group × window interaction *F*(3,90) = 12.37, *p* < .0001, ηp² = .29, with comprehension equated (89% vs 88%) ([Rayner, Slattery & Bélanger 2010](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/PBR.17.6.834.pdf)). Span tracks *language ability*, not oculomotor speed ([Choi et al. 2015](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/s13414-015-0942-1.pdf)). **A fixed global rate ignores a ~60% spread in how much text a reader can use per glance.** Rayner et al. draw the conclusion themselves: speed-reading devices "may need to be tailored to each individual user."

Skipping is **not discarding** — when words that most people skip are deleted from a passage, "comprehension suffers rather dramatically" ([Rayner et al. 2016](http://faculty.cas.usf.edu/eschotter/papers/Rayner_Schotter_Masson_Potter_Treiman_2016_PSPI.pdf)).

**The isolated test of regression-removal is the strongest single result in this dossier.** [Schotter, Tran & Rayner 2014](http://faculty.cas.usf.edu/eschotter/papers/Schotter_Tran_Rayner_2014_PsychSci.pdf) (*Psych. Science* 25(6), N = 40, EyeLink 1000 @ 1 kHz) used a trailing mask — each word's letters became x's once the eyes passed — which removes regressions while leaving fixation control and preview intact. Comprehension: **84%** (normal reading with a regression), 78% (normal, no regression, n.s.), **71%** (trailing mask, *b* = −0.75, *z* = 4.03, *p* < .001), **56%** on the rare trials where masked readers regressed anyway (24 of 1,409, <2%; the authors flag this cell as underpowered). (**Chance on these yes/no items is 50%**, and the stimuli were 20 garden-path + 20 unambiguous single sentences among 40 fillers, Courier New 12 pt at 2.99 chars/degree, 60 cm — so the 56% cell is at chance and "84%" is sentence-level question accuracy, not prose comprehension.) Main effect of display *b* = −0.92, *z* = 5.09, *p* < .001; the interaction with sentence type was only **marginal** (*b* = 0.70, *z* = 1.90, *p* = .06) — the loss is **global, not confined to garden-path sentences**. Initial gaze duration on the critical noun was unaffected, so readers did not change their initial strategy; they simply **stopped attempting repairs** (*b* = −3.10, *z* = 7.94, *p* < .001). That suppression is the silent-failure signature: comprehension falls without the reader signalling anything went wrong.

A converging result from a different method: [Blott, Rodd & Warren 2023](https://doi.org/10.1080/23273798.2022.2159992) (*Language, Cognition and Neuroscience* 38(6):779–801) compared Spoken / Written / RSVP presentation of lexically ambiguous sentences and found **reinterpretation-related processing costs exaggerated in RSVP**, in both response times and left inferior frontal gyrus activation. Abstract verified verbatim during audit: "Reinterpretation-related processing costs were exaggerated in the RSVP format, both for response times on a behavioural task and neural activation in left inferior frontal gyrus," closing with a warning aimed at the field itself — "many neuroimaging studies of reading present sentences in an **artificial, cognitively demanding** word-by-word rapid serial visual presentation (RSVP) format … we urge language researchers to carefully consider the role of presentation format." **Still SECONDHAND at the numeric level** — full text is paywalled, so N, effect sizes and rates were not retrieved.

An RSVP "go back" button is **not** equivalent: existing ones "merely start the text (or sentence) over from the beginning", and it has not been shown that they are as effective as eyes "in which readers have a spatial representation of the text and a sense of where to move" ([Rayner et al. 2016](http://faculty.cas.usf.edu/eschotter/papers/Rayner_Schotter_Masson_Potter_Treiman_2016_PSPI.pdf)). A principled auto-regress is **not derivable from the literature**: "Regression behavior in reading is one of the less understood aspects of the process, so even generating a general principle for this would be difficult."

**What RSVP does not remove, in RSVP's favour:** reading is not reconstructable from parafoveal vision. Masking even a single foveal letter halves reading rate, and word identification hits chance ~3° from fixation ([Rayner et al. 2016](http://faculty.cas.usf.edu/eschotter/papers/Rayner_Schotter_Masson_Potter_Treiman_2016_PSPI.pdf); [Schotter et al. 2012](http://faculty.cas.usf.edu/eschotter/papers/Schotter_Angele_Rayner_2012_APP.pdf)). That kills the "read a whole line at a glance" family of claims and is **neutral-to-favourable** for RSVP, which keeps every word foveal by construction.

**The drop-off curve is much shallower in nominal wpm than in delivered wpm.** Di Nocera's Table 1 — which I extracted and verified from the PDF directly — shows the Spritz-derived engine delivered **195 / 230 / 265 / 305 / 344 actual wpm** for nominal **250 / 300 / 350 / 400 / 450**, against a control that read at **226 wpm**. So the entire tested RSVP range topped out at **344 delivered wpm — a 52% speed gain bought with a 12-point comprehension loss**, and only 14 points above the **53%** scored by 30 students who never read the passage.

**Compare the same reader with eyes intact.** [Schwalm, Radach & Kuperman 2026](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10888438.2025.2612649) (*Scientific Studies of Reading*, N = 46, speed imposed by within-page line enhancement rather than by removing eye movements, 225/270/350/360/405 wpm) report comprehension consistent across the range with a **significant decline only at 405 wpm**, eye movements adapting efficiently while "word length, frequency and surprisal effects remained fully intact." **UNVERIFIED at the numeric level** — Taylor & Francis is Cloudflare-blocked; the specific speed list comes from the article page rendering, not a parsed PDF. [Vibert, Colas & Danion 2025](https://hal.science/hal-05001737/document) (N = 30, Tobii TX300, 15 three-sentence texts of 59–70 words at 38.0/19.0/9.5/6.3 s or free) give the page-reading curve: **86.7 / 80.6 / 66.8 / 48.9%**, almost perfectly linear in imposed speed (*y* = −0.073*x* + 95.2, **r = −.996**) — i.e. **~7.3 points lost per 100 wpm imposed**. Their actual gaze-derived speed ceilinged at **359 wpm** even when 623 was demanded; readers met the deadline by *not reading the whole text*.

**The gap between those two curves is the cost of removing the oculomotor system.**

### The counter-case this dossier previously suppressed

The first draft cited [Rubin & Turano 1992](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1604858/) (*Vision Research* 32(5):895–902) **only** for its 69.4 ms recognition floor, and omitted the rest of the same abstract. In full: 13 normally-sighted observers read PAGE vs RSVP text; RSVP was faster at every letter size tested, and at 8× single-letter acuity the averages were **1171 wpm RSVP vs 303 wpm PAGE (oral reading, Expt 1)**. In Expt 2 (silent reading, multiple-choice test after each passage) **6 of 13 subjects read at the apparatus maximum of 1652 wpm with at least 75% correct**. The authors' conclusion is the pro-RSVP thesis stated plainly: "the programming and execution of saccadic eye movements impose an upper limit on conventional reading speed."

**Why it does not license 1000 wpm in a PDF reader, stated with the conditions:**

- 1171 wpm is **oral reading rate scored as correct words per minute** — a decoding measure, the same construct as Primativo's word-report thresholds, not comprehension.
- **1652 wpm was the ceiling of the apparatus, not of the readers** — the abstract says "the maximum testable rate", so that cell is censored and cannot be read as a measured limit.
- The comprehension instrument, sample (n = 13), passage type and chance level are not recoverable from the abstract; **UNVERIFIED at the item level** — the full text is paywalled and no OA copy was found.
- It is **34 years old**, CRT-era, and its own successors read it narrowly: [Schwalm, Radach & Kuperman 2026](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/10888438.2025.2612649) cite it as "median reading rates of 790 wpm, with the fastest participants reaching 1652 wpm. **However, comprehension declines significantly when longer sentences or paragraphs are presented in this manner** (Juola et al., 1982; Potter, 1980), limiting the utility of RSVP for theoretical explorations of maximum reading speed" — verified verbatim in the retrieved PDF.

**The honest summary is a dissociation, not a contradiction: RSVP genuinely removes a decoding-rate bottleneck, and the comprehension bottleneck it does not remove binds first at every length above a sentence.** Any product claim built on Rubin & Turano is a claim about how fast you can *pronounce* text.

### The working-memory bottleneck

[Primativo et al. 2016](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0153786) (*PLOS ONE* 11(4):e0153786; six experiments, Italian, adaptive QUEST staircase to 80%-correct word report) decompose the ceiling. **Conditions, which the numbers below are worthless without: *n* = 6 per experiment** (every reported ANOVA is *F*(2,10)), **reading aloud**, 57 cm from a 15.5″ laptop at 60 Hz, Italian stimuli, no comprehension measure of any kind. Isolated decoding approaches **1200 wpm**; "when the number of stimuli exceeded the short-term memory span, RR decreased to **800 wpm**"; semantic context contributes only a factor of 1.4; and **eye movements impose a ceiling around 300 wpm**. Crucially, **memory load and stream length degrade rates directly**: 12-word ordered trials ran at **524 wpm (115 ms/word)** vs 4-word streams at 1239 wpm. The authors state plainly: "Text comprehension was not evaluated since it goes beyond the scope of the present work" — **these are word-report thresholds, not comprehension**, and should never be quoted as achievable reading rates.

[Acklin & Papesh](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29461715/) found **WMC did not predict comprehension** — the bottleneck is not individual span capacity but the fixed conveyor. Their paper drew a published rebuttal ([Juola 2018](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.5406/amerjpsyc.131.2.0225)), so the interpretation is contested inside the field — though Juola's own group subsequently produced the Di Nocera data consistent with a cost above ~300 nominal wpm.

### Conflicts

**(a) Di Nocera contradicts itself.** Bonferroni-corrected post-hocs show no cost up to nominal 350; the authors' own uncorrected single comparisons find **control vs 300 (*p* = .023, ηp² = .074) and control vs 350 (*p* = .008, ηp² = .097)** both significant — the latter being the *largest* reported effect. Their discussion hedges to "some limits... at presentation rates higher than 300 wpm". **Do not treat 350 nominal wpm as a validated safe operating point.**

**(b) Di Nocera glosses Boo & Conklin misleadingly (corrected during audit — the earlier draft of this section overstated the error).** Di Nocera's introduction states Boo & Conklin found RSVP "did not impair inferential comprehension for both native and non-native speakers when subjects read at 500 wpm, supporting Spritz efficacy" — verified verbatim in the extracted PDF. **The narrow factual claim is actually correct**: in Boo & Conklin's data the natives' gist item at 500 wpm went 80% → 60%, *z* = −1.13, *p* = .26, n.s. What is wrong is the gloss. In the same table, overall comprehension fell 81% → 57% and detail 82% → 53% (*z* = −2.78, *p* = .01), and the [primary paper's abstract](https://pops.lancashire.ac.uk/index.php/jsltr/article/view/334/137) reads: "reading comprehension was impaired when single words were presented at rates of 500-wpm and 1000-wpm." **The failure mode to watch is not fabrication but selective quotation of the one null in a table of significant losses** — and that is exactly how 500 wpm acquires an evidence base in secondary literature and vendor citations.

**A caveat that cuts the other way, in Di Nocera's favour:** Boo & Conklin's gist score rests on **one binary-scored item per text** (each 5-question set was 1 gist + 4 detail, three response options, verified in the PDF's Methods and Appendix). With a single item per condition per participant, SDs are .41–.52 and the gist null is close to uninterpretable. **Do not cite "gist is spared" from this study as a positive finding; cite only "detail was lost."**

**(c) Matched-rate parity depends on an artefact.** Both Potter et al. and Juola et al. inserted **a pause between each sentence**, "which would have allowed comprehension processes to catch up"; [Masson 1983](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.3758/bf03196973) showed that **without** those pauses, RSVP fell below skimming at the same duration ([via PSPI](http://faculty.cas.usf.edu/eschotter/papers/Rayner_Schotter_Masson_Potter_Treiman_2016_PSPI.pdf)). Any claim of "RSVP equals normal reading" carries this asterisk.

**(d) Population matters.** [Moussaoui et al. 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/identifier/S1355617725101628/type/journal_article) is the one study where RSVP *wins*, and only for ADHD readers at ~200 wpm. Neurotypical controls got **worse** (−5.82%) in the same experiment — consistent with everything else here.

**The literature is small and has largely stopped.** An OpenAlex sweep returns ~42 works on RSVP + reading comprehension since 2016, of which the direct rate-vs-comprehension experiments are Hester 2016, Acklin & Papesh 2017, Di Nocera 2018 and Ricciardi 2020. My own 2021+ sweep added only the ADHD study and Blott et al. **The entire case rests on roughly six experiments**, four with per-cell N of 15–35, plus 43–46-year-old CRT-era matched-rate comparisons.

## 4. Where RSVP measurably fails

**Dense technical text — the decisive result.** Just, Masson & Carpenter (1980), reported in [Rayner et al. 2016](http://faculty.cas.usf.edu/eschotter/papers/Rayner_Schotter_Masson_Potter_Treiman_2016_PSPI.pdf): trained speed readers (600–700 wpm) beat instructed skimmers on gist but not on detail, and neither could answer detail questions "if they had not fixated on the regions where the answers were located." Then: **"when the three groups of participants were given more technical texts (taken from *Scientific American*), for which background knowledge would be very sparse, the speed readers no longer showed an advantage over the skimmers, even on general questions."** The fast-reading advantage is attributed to *extended inferencing* — constructing inferences from partial input plus prior knowledge. **That is precisely what a reader of an unfamiliar academic paper does not have.**

**Verbatim/literal detail.** The dissociation is the most replicated finding in this dossier and the one that matters most for a numeric-fidelity reader: [Benedetto](https://www.tsw.it/wp-content/uploads/Rapid-serial-visual-presentation-in-reading-The-case-of-Spritz-1.pdf) literal 60% vs 72% (ηp² = .12) with inferential null (ηp² = .02) **at zero speed gain**; [Boo & Conklin](https://pops.lancashire.ac.uk/index.php/jsltr/article/view/334/137) detail 82% → 53% with gist n.s.; [Acklin & Papesh](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29461715/) slower RSVP → better verbatim. Benedetto's interpretation: inferential comprehension **compensates** using long-term memory for a degraded literal representation — **the reader will feel they understood the paper and be unable to report the numbers in it.**

**Silent word substitution.** Potter, Moryadas, Abrams & Noel (1993), via [PSPI](http://faculty.cas.usf.edu/eschotter/papers/Rayner_Schotter_Masson_Potter_Treiman_2016_PSPI.pdf): sentences like "The sailor washed the *dack* of that vessel" at **10 words/second (100 ms/word, ~600 wpm)**, with participants **explicitly instructed to write down exactly what they saw, including misspellings**. They misreported the nonword "dack" as "deck" **40%** of the time and the wrong real word "duck" as "deck" **26%** of the time. The mechanism: "a largely unconscious process combines letter-level information and the sentence context... with a bias toward actual words and toward words that are plausible in context." **The substitution survives explicit warning and comes with no awareness of error.**

**Long sentences and re-reading needs.** Four converging strands, none of which is a direct manipulation of sentence length under RSVP — that specific experiment does not exist in anything retrieved:

1. **Regression removal costs comprehension globally, not just on hard sentences.** [Schotter, Tran & Rayner 2014](http://faculty.cas.usf.edu/eschotter/papers/Schotter_Tran_Rayner_2014_PsychSci.pdf): 84% → 71%, sentence-type interaction only marginal (*b* = 0.70, *z* = 1.90, *p* = .06). Their own framing of the mechanism is the relevant part for long sentences — readers "stopped attempting repairs" (*b* = −3.10, *z* = 7.94, *p* < .001) rather than failing at them.
2. **Reinterpretation specifically is worse under RSVP than under either speech or page text**, behaviourally and in left IFG ([Blott, Rodd & Warren 2023](https://doi.org/10.1080/23273798.2022.2159992)). Long sentences are where late-arriving disambiguation lives.
3. **Length effects on the RSVP side are structural, not just cognitive.** The [Spritz patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20140016867A1/en)'s own inter-sentence blank scales with sentence length (×1.0 / 2.2 / 3.3), and [Di Nocera et al.](https://iris.uniroma1.it/retrieve/e3835320-f382-15e8-e053-a505fe0a3de9/Di-Nocera_Rapid_2018.pdf) confirm the shipped engine's actual rate "is affected by factors such as word and sentence lengths" — which is why nominal wpm and delivered wpm diverge by 20–25%. A long sentence is therefore *also* the case where a nominal rate lies most.
4. **The one experiment that put slack inside long sentences made things worse.** [Rahman & Muter 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10354807/) — see §6.

**The honest gap:** every RSVP comprehension study retrieved used passages of ordinary prose (409–619 words for the short ones, 5,804–6,169 for the long ones) and **none reported comprehension as a function of sentence length**. The claim "RSVP fails on long sentences" is *mechanistically well-supported and directly untested* — mark it as such wherever it is used to justify a design choice.

**Central vision loss — RSVP loses where its theory says it should win.** [Journal of Vision 21(12):5 (2021)](https://jov.arvojournals.org/arvo/content_public/journal/jov/938565/i1534-7362-21-12-5_1636451734.57769.pdf): with simulated and with actual macular-degeneration scotomas, "scrolling text produced the highest comprehension and lowest error rates... RSVP produced lower overall comprehension and high error rates." Adherence to eccentric viewing was *better* with RSVP but "did not translate into better reading performance", and participants "consistently adopted a strategy of making multiple horizontal saccades on the text itself" — **the oculomotor system fights the format.**

**Numerals, tables, equations, citations — no evidence exists.** Across every study retrieved by four research passes, stimuli were: an Italian magazine article, Orwell's *1984* in French, IELTS expository passages, Harry Potter paragraphs, Bransford-Johnson paragraphs, garden-path sentences, Italian *Alice in Wonderland* and word lists, and unspecified grade-level passages. **Not one used scientific prose with numbers, symbols, subscripts, inline citations or tabular data.** I ran two further OpenAlex sweeps for eye-movement studies of numerals in running text and got zero hits. The closest available proxy is [Primativo et al. 2016](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0153786): random word lists (no sentential context) needed roughly **2× the dwell** of ordered text at the same accuracy criterion — 588 vs 1239 wpm unmasked, 269 vs 524 wpm in 12-word memory-loaded trials (**223 vs 114 ms/word**) — **but that proxy rests on *n* = 6 Italian readers reading aloud, with no comprehension measure**, so it is a starting multiplier, not a finding. Whether digit strings are *more* robust (no lexical attractor to snap to) or *less* (no contextual redundancy, no parafoveal length/shape cue) is **UNESTABLISHED**.

**Repetition blindness — a second silent-failure mechanism, specific to RSVP and absent from the first draft.** In RSVP sentences, the second occurrence of a repeated word is frequently not reported, *and the omission does not respect grammar*: "When she spilled the ink there was ink all over," presented at ~100 ms/word, is reported as "When she spilled the ink there was all over" ([Kanwisher 1987](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0277(87)90016-3), described in [Bond & Andrews 2008, *Memory & Cognition* 36(2):295–313](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/MC.36.2.295.pdf)). Bond & Andrews' Experiment 3 (N = 22, every word centred for **117 ms** — i.e. **~513 wpm**, squarely the marketed RSVP range) measured an **RB effect of 29.6% for congruous sentences** (*F*(1,21) = 30.84) and **24.0% for incongruous** (*F*(1,21) = 21.97), with repetition and congruity **additive, not interacting** (*F*(1,21) = 1.55, *p* = .227). Their account is lexical: "reactivation and identification of rapidly repeated words are impaired due to the refractory nature of lexical representations." **Conditions and limits, stated honestly:** these are *whole-sentence report* tasks with an unusual double-word display, not comprehension tests; the demonstrations cluster at 100–120 ms/word; and **whether RB survives at the 200–350 ms/word range a real reader would use is not established by anything retrieved here — UNVERIFIED.** What makes it decision-relevant anyway is the failure signature: like Potter's substitutions, the reader produces a degraded output with no sense that anything was dropped. For academic prose, the repeated tokens are exactly the load-bearing ones — a term repeated across a clause, a repeated unit, a variable name appearing twice in a sentence.

**Evaluation floors are high.** Di Nocera's 49-item T/F questionnaire scored **53%** among 30 students who never read the passage (verified in the PDF); [Carver's parallel result](http://faculty.cas.usf.edu/eschotter/papers/Rayner_Schotter_Masson_Potter_Treiman_2016_PSPI.pdf) was **57%** from common knowledge and guessing. Any in-house evaluation of an RSVP feature **must include a no-read control group** or it is measuring prior knowledge.

## 5. ORP / pivot letter: the claim, the literature, and the evidence

**The claim's origin is marketing.** Spritz's archived "The Science" page states: "For each word, the eye seeks a certain point within the word, which we call the 'Optimal Recognition Point' or ORP. After your eyes find the ORP, your brain starts to process the meaning of the word," and that with traditional RSVP "when words are not positioned on the ORP, your eyes search for it" ([archived 2015-12-26](http://web.archive.org/web/20151226070105/http://spritzinc.com/the-science)). **Neither that page nor the companion blog post contains a single citation.** The FAQ concedes on the transfer claim: "People keep telling us that it does, but we haven't conducted any studies in this area yet" ([archived 2015-10-08](http://web.archive.org/web/20151008011808/http://www.spritzinc.com/faq/)).

**The patent admits most of its table is interpolation.** [US 8,903,174 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8903174B2/en) (*Serial text display for optimal recognition apparatus and method*, Spritz Holding LLC, granted 2 Dec 2014) states verbatim: "Brysbaert and Nazir had provided recommendations for the ORP character position only for words of **3, 5, 7, and 9** characters in length," and "a ratio based on the ORP character position was created in order to **interpolate** between the established values … and to **extrapolate** for words of **10-13** characters in length," with the values for **4, 6, 8, and 10-13** chosen "by keeping the ORP character position ratio between **0.20 to 0.33**." TABLE I "ranges from 3 characters to preferably 13 characters, but not more than 20 characters."

*(Correction made during audit: an earlier draft of this section said "12 of 18 tabulated lengths." That number is not derivable from the patent text. The defensible statement is: **the patent claims outside authority for four word lengths — 3, 5, 7, 9 — and generated the rest itself**, explicitly by ratio-fitting rather than by measurement.)*

**The patent also asserts an unpublished validation, which should be recorded and discounted.** Same paragraph: the resulting ORP positions "were empirically tested and confirmed by the inventors with **20 subjects** utilizing texts displayed according to this embodiment." No design, measure, comparison condition, or result is given, and no corresponding publication was found. **Treat this as a claim in a commercial filing, not as evidence** — note in particular that it describes confirming the ORP *table*, not comparing ORP alignment against centring, which is the contrast that matters and the one [Kosch et al.](https://thomaskosch.com/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/kosch2020one.pdf) later ran and found null.

(The Brysbaert & Nazir claim itself remains **UNVERIFIED** — the 2005 paper was not retrieved in this pass either; the only source for what B&N recommended is the patent, an interested party.)

**The geometric magnitude is tiny.** The patent's zero-based index reduces to `ceil((L−1)/4)`, capped at 4. Displacement from plain geometric centring: **0 chars at L=3, 0.5 at L=4, 1.0 at L=5, 1.5 at L=8, 2.0 at L=9, 3.0 at L=13**. Since mean English running-text word length is ~4–5 characters, **for the modal word ORP alignment shifts the pivot by roughly half a character to one character relative to simply centring it.**

**The underlying construct is measured in a paradigm that does not resemble RSVP.** The optimal viewing position is established with **isolated words, ~150 ms exposure, the eye forced to a chosen letter, lexical decision or naming** ([Li et al. 2017, *Psychology and Aging*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5459221/)). It answers "if I force your eye to letter *k* of an isolated flashed word, how fast do you decide it is a word?" — not "where should I put a coloured letter in a word displayed alone at a fixed point your eye is already fixating." Note also the nomenclature slip: the **preferred viewing location** (where eyes actually land, ~halfway between word beginning and middle) is a *descriptive oculomotor fact*, distinct from the OVP (a *performance optimum under forced fixation*). Spritz's copy conflates all three.

**The direct test of the OVP premise *in reading* comes out backwards.** [Vitu, McConkie, Kerr & O'Regan 2001, *Vision Research* 41(25–26):3513–3533](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11718792/) analysed **three large existing corpora of eye-movement data — two adult, one child** — asking whether the isolated-word OVP effect shows up as a relationship between fixation position within a word and fixation duration. It does not; it inverts: "a surprising **inverted Optimal Viewing Position curve: mean fixation duration is greatest, rather than lowest, when the eyes were at the centers of words**." The authors note the phenomenon "contradicts expectations of both oculomotor and cognitive theories of eye movement control," and separately report a **Saccade Distance effect** (fixation durations are longer the further the previous fixation was). This is the strongest single piece of evidence against the ORP mechanism *as a mechanism*, and it comes from O'Regan's own group — the same lab as the 1984 OVP paper the whole ORP story is built on. **Caveat, stated plainly: it does not test RSVP.** It shows that in normal reading the word position the ORP heuristic optimises for is associated with the *longest* dwell, so the ORP story's causal claim ("the eye seeks the ORP; once found, processing begins") does not describe reading.

**The leading review rebuts the premise directly.** [Rayner et al. 2016](http://faculty.cas.usf.edu/eschotter/papers/Rayner_Schotter_Masson_Potter_Treiman_2016_PSPI.pdf): "readers are capable of identifying words whether or not they look at the OVP; there is just a slight loss of processing efficiency... readers begin to process words before looking directly at them, rather than 'once the ORP is found.'" They concede exactly one narrower point: centring at the OVP "may be an improvement over RSVP methods in which words are **left justified**", because left justification puts different-length words' OVPs at different screen positions — "when words are centered... the eye will remain close to the OVP." **The defensible claim is ORP > left-justified. The marketed claim is ORP > centred, and that is the one tested and found null.**

**The one controlled test.** [Kosch, Schmidt, Thanheiser & Chuang, CHI 2020](https://thomaskosch.com/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/kosch2020one.pdf) (DOI [10.1145/3313831.3376766](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3313831.3376766)) crossed **Text Alignment {centred, ORP-aligned uncoloured, ORP-aligned red}** × **Speed {200, 350, 500 WPM}**, within-subjects, balanced Latin square, N = 18 recruited / 17 analysed, ten ~548-word excerpts with 10 comprehension questions each, 32-channel BrainVision LiveAmp EEG, 27″ display at ~50 cm, 55 pt RSVP. I extracted their Table 1 from the PDF directly:

**Nobody has replicated or overturned it in six years.** A Semantic Scholar citation sweep of the CHI paper (40 citing works, 2020–2026) surfaces no re-test of ORP alignment or highlighting. The 2024 state of the art simply restates it: "the ORP is not a decisive factor influencing the perceived mental workload" ([Grootjen, Thalhammer & Kosch 2024](https://www.medien.ifi.lmu.de/pubdb/publications/pub/grootjen2024youreyesonspeed/grootjen2024youreyesonspeed.pdf)) — that paper did **not** itself manipulate ORP.

**The patent's mechanical premise is empirically false.** [Benedetto et al. 2015](https://www.tsw.it/wp-content/uploads/Rapid-serial-visual-presentation-in-reading-The-case-of-Spritz-1.pdf) measured saccade rate **0.27 Hz with Spritz vs 2.17 Hz traditional** (*F*(1,50)=311.80, *p*<.001, ηp²=.86): "Spritz reduces saccades **without really suppressing them**" — an ~87% reduction, roughly one saccade every 3.7 s even with every ORP pinned to the same pixel. Fixation duration was 553 ms with **SD 294** — a coefficient of variation over 0.5, not the signature of one steady pivot.

**Implementations disagree about what the ORP even is.** *(Counts corrected during audit — the first draft said "four of ten / six of ten," which does not match its own table.)* The table below lists the patent plus **nine shipped implementations: four reproduce the patent rule, five invent their own.** Three rows were re-fetched and re-read during this audit: [Miserlou/OpenSpritz](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Miserlou/OpenSpritz/master/spritz.js) `pivot()` is a switch giving 1-based 2 for L=2–5 and 3 for L=6–9 — patent-equivalent, confirmed; [cameron/squirt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cameron/squirt/master/squirt.js) is literally `length <= 1 ? 0 : (length == 2 ? 1 : (length == 3 ? 1 : Math.floor(length / 2) - 1))` with trailing punctuation stripped from the length first, unbounded — confirmed; [pasky/speedread](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pasky/speedread/master/speedread) carries `$ORPloc = 0.35; $ORPmax = 0.2;` — a *third* rule, proportional rather than tabular, which the first draft's timing table cited but its ORP table omitted.

**Dingler et al. also contain the only retrieved practice data, which the first draft missed.** In their lab study each participant read four texts in four rounds at **150% of their own measured baseline wpm**; RSVP comprehension rose from **4.58/10 (SD 1.43) in round 1 to 6.0/10 (SD 1.65) in round 4**, *t*(11) = 2.75, *p* = .01, *r* = .64 (the kinetic-pacer group improved comparably). Their eye tracking during RSVP is also worth recording: participants still made **118 fixations per text with a mean fixation duration of 1044 ms (SD 352)** — independent corroboration of Benedetto's "reduces saccades without really suppressing them." **Limits: n = 12 per cell, one 60-minute session, no counterbalancing of text against round reported, and no no-practice control**, so this is a within-session adaptation effect, not evidence that the RSVP comprehension deficit closes with training. It is enough, though, that "no practice evidence exists" is too strong a claim — see §8.

**A widely repeated supporting null is a citation-chain error.** Both [Kosch et al.](https://thomaskosch.com/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/kosch2020one.pdf) and [Grootjen et al.](https://www.medien.ifi.lmu.de/pubdb/publications/pub/grootjen2024youreyesonspeed/grootjen2024youreyesonspeed.pdf) attribute an "underlined vs coloured ORP" comparison to [Dingler et al. AH'15](https://kaikunze.de/papers/pdf/dingler2015assessment.pdf). Dingler et al. actually compared a **moving-underline kinetic pacer over full page text** against **Squirt-based RSVP with a blue pivot** — two different reading modes, not two ORP renderings. Their nulls are real (N=24 between-subjects, mental load *t*(22)=.889, *p*=.38; comprehension *t*(22)=.62, *p*=.40) but answer a different question. **No published study has compared an underlined ORP to a coloured one.**

### Conflicts

**The two canonical Rayner reviews disagree by an order of magnitude on OVP size, citing the same 1984 source.** [Rayner 1998](http://andrewd.ces.clemson.edu/courses/cpsc881/papers/reading/Ray98_readingSurvey.pdf): "For every letter that the participant's fixation deviates from the optimal viewing position, the associated cost amounts to **about 20 ms** (O'Regan et al., 1984)" — with [Ganayim 2016](https://journals.pan.pl/Content/99933/download/) giving 20–30 ms/letter from the same source. [Rayner et al. 2016](http://faculty.cas.usf.edu/eschotter/papers/Rayner_Schotter_Masson_Potter_Treiman_2016_PSPI.pdf), same citation: "it is small, on the order of only **a few milliseconds**." I did not retrieve O'Regan et al. (1984) and cannot adjudicate — **report both**. The most likely reconciliation is that the steep slope is from the forced-fixation isolated-word paradigm and the "few ms" characterises what survives into ecological conditions. **Both reviews agree on the load-bearing conclusion**: in *text* reading the effect is "greatly attenuated or absent" (1998) / "even smaller" (2016). This matters because an ORP advocate can legitimately quote 20 ms/letter from Rayner's own review — and it does not survive into text.

## 6. Timing models

### Why fixed-interval is wrong

[Rayner, Slattery, Drieghe & Liversedge 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3543826/) (N=28, EyeLink 2000, 14 pt Consolas at 3.2 letters/deg, 87% comprehension) crossed word length (4–6 / 7–9 / 10–12 letters) with predictability. **Adjusted gaze duration** (skipped words scored 0 — the true average time a word costs) ran **136 / 162, 168 / 193, 184 / 221 ms** — a **1.63× range on length × predictability alone**, before frequency or sentence position. Skipping ran 36/28%, 22/18%, 18/10%.

**But the mechanism is refixation, not longer looks.** First-fixation duration was **flat**: 205/209, 193/204, 195/211 ms — the long-unpredictable value is 2 ms above short-unpredictable. The whole 24 ms gaze increase is carried by **+0.12 fixations**. **Extra RSVP dwell on a long word is not reproducing a longer fixation (there isn't one) — it is substituting for a second fixation the reader can no longer make.** Calibrate against gaze/adjusted-gaze, never first-fixation.

| [Warren, White & Reichle 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2682724/) | punctuation: period > comma > none on every measure | *F* climbs from first-fixation (6.8) to regressions-out (25.1) |
| [Payne & Stine-Morrow 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3376187/) | sentence-final vs internal, young adults | **+26 ms first fixation, +48 ms gaze, +121 ms regression-path** |

**A 10-bit surprisal swing — roughly "the" vs a rare technical term — buys only 20–40 ms.** Anyone scaling dwell by frequency over a 5:1 range is over-applying the effect by an order of magnitude. (In tension: [Pimentel et al. 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.1052.pdf) argue the relation may be slightly *superlinear*, γ ∈ [1, 1.5]; unsettled.)

**Sentence wrap-up is mostly a regression effect, not a longer forward dwell.** [Warren et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2682724/) found punctuation did **not** interact with syntactic complexity (all *F*s ≤ 1, *p*s > .3), concluding "traditional accounts of clause wrap-up are incomplete", and their E-Z Reader fit reproduced the data by raising integration **failure** probability (0.26/0.42/0.62 for null/comma/period) while integration **time fell** (102/84/75 ms). **A pause at a period buys back a regression RSVP forbids — that is why implemented pauses of 2–5.7× look excessive against first-pass data yet defensible against total-time data.** Wrap-up survives extreme time pressure: [Vibert et al. 2025](https://hal.science/hal-05001737/document) still measured +22 ms on sentence-final fixations at 623 wpm imposed (*d* = .37), alongside 7.7% regressions retained and 77.1% of content words still fixated.

### The recognition-time floor

**Not 69 ms.** [Rubin & Turano 1992](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1604858/) (*Vision Research* 32(5), 13 observers) measured a minimum word duration for accurate oral reading of **69.4 ms**, "and was not reduced by increasing ISI" — **inserting blank gaps between words does not buy identification time.** But that is isolated word decoding. The practical floor for connected prose in a memory-loaded stream is **~115–223 ms/word**: [Primativo et al.](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0153786) 12-word ordered trials ran at 524 wpm (**115 ms/word**) and random word lists at 269 wpm (**223 ms/word**). [Legge, Mansfield & Chung 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11248262/) add a visual constraint: central-vision visual spans reach peak amplitude "in 100 ms or less", so **below ~100 ms a word wider than the fovea is partly lost**.

**A blank gap does not buy identification time, but it does buy attentional recovery — two different levels, and the first draft conflated them.** Rubin & Turano's "not reduced by increasing ISI" is about the *minimum exposure needed to decode a word*. It says nothing about the attentional blink: [Raymond, Shapiro & Arnell 1992](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.18.3.849) (*JEP:HPP* 18(3):849) found that in an RSVP stream, a probe is "poorly detected when presented during a **270-ms interval beginning 180 ms after the target**," while probes immediately after the target or later in the stream are detected accurately — and, critically, "**this temporary reduction in probe detection was not found in conditions in which a brief blank interval followed the target**." **Conditions: letters, not words; a detection task, not reading.** Whether the blink applies word-to-word in continuous prose is not established by that paper and is **UNVERIFIED here**. But the mechanism is the best available account of why an inter-word gap or a punctuation pause helps for reasons unrelated to decoding time, and it makes the blank-gap parameter worth exposing rather than hard-coding to zero.

**Words mid-stream are masked by their neighbours.** [Primativo et al.](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0153786) Exp 2 (unmasked 4-word streams): word 1 = 1365 wpm, word 2 = 696, word 3 = **496**, word 4 = 1787. Interior words are forward- *and* backward-masked and are ~4× slower than edges, an effect occurring "at an early stage of processing (either features or letters) before the words are lexically encoded." They further note temporal crowding is demonstrated at **SOAs of 200–330 ms** — precisely the dwell range every real RSVP reader operates in. **Part of the benefit of a punctuation pause is perceptual (it unmasks neighbours), not cognitive.**

### The upper bound nobody designs against

[Rahman & Muter 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10354807/) (*Human Factors* 41(1)) set each participant's within-sentence RSVP rate to their own normal page-reading speed — "poor efficiency resulted." Fixing the within-sentence rate at ~260 wpm for everyone and moving all self-pacing to a **between-sentence key press plus a sentence-repeat key** restored efficiency to page-format parity. Their conclusion: **"slower reading in RSVP should be achieved by increasing pauses between sentences or by repeating sentences, not by decreasing the within-sentence presentation rate"** — because a word left up too long invites the reader to make eye movements around it. Caveat: one 1999 study, effect inferred not eye-tracked, CRT-era, at a rate below modern defaults.

### The only direct test of variable dwell

[Castelhano & Muter 2001](https://web.archive.org/web/20071211155000/http://www.psych.utoronto.ca:80/users/muter/castelhanoandmuter.htm) (*Behaviour & IT* 20(4), base 230 ms/word) tested a completion meter, **halved dwell (115 ms) for 11 high-frequency function words**, and **doubled dwell (460 ms) at punctuation**. Punctuation pauses were liked and differed significantly from the other two (*F*(2,38)=6.84, *p*<.01); the frequency shortening scored **neutral**, and the authors suspected it "may have in effect aggravated" the problem and that varying dwell "may have decreased the predictability of word presentation, making reading from RSVP more uncomfortable." Experiment 2 combined punctuation pauses, 2× clause-initial dwell, and a pause key: **significantly preferred, significantly SLOWER than both plain RSVP and normal page (*F*(2,250)=26.36, *p*<.01), and no comprehension difference (*F*(2,250)=2.53, *p*>.05).**

**Bottom line on timing: variable dwell bought comfort, not comprehension, and cost speed.** Nothing since has tested variable-duration RSVP against constant-duration RSVP **at a matched mean delivered rate with a comprehension outcome** — Di Nocera et al. name this as the open question and it is still open. The 2026 state of the art is [Shanthi, Shende & Rakshith](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-19318-6_27), which uses spaCy POS-tagging and NER to set delays and reports only "preliminary evaluations" with no rates or comprehension results in the abstract. Its arXiv predecessor [Thoth (arXiv:1908.01699)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.01699) has a two-sentence "RESULTS" section with **no participants, no speeds, no comprehension measures, no statistics** — and a summarisation pass over that PDF fabricated an entire study for it (15 participants, 250/400/600 wpm, "75–85% accuracy"). **Any RSVP timing claim sourced through a summariser must be checked against the parsed PDF.**

## 7. Design implications for this project

- **Default to ≤300 wpm delivered, and label the number as delivered, not nominal.** Costs appear at 230–305 delivered wpm in the only dose-response study ([Di Nocera Table 1, verified](https://iris.uniroma1.it/retrieve/e3835320-f382-15e8-e053-a505fe0a3de9/Di-Nocera_Rapid_2018.pdf)); Spritz-family engines deliver **20–25% below nominal**. A UI that shows "450 wpm" while delivering 344 is lying to the user and mis-citing the literature.
- **Do not ship 500+ wpm as a supported reading mode; ship it as skimming and say so.** Detail comprehension collapses to 53% at 500 wpm and going to 1000 costs nothing more ([Boo & Conklin](https://pops.lancashire.ac.uk/index.php/jsltr/article/view/334/137)); static text beats RSVP outright at 700 and 1000 ([Acklin & Papesh](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29461715/)).
- **Treat numeric/verbatim content as the primary hazard and route around it.** Literal comprehension degrades while inferential does not, **even at zero speed gain** ([Benedetto](https://www.tsw.it/wp-content/uploads/Rapid-serial-visual-presentation-in-reading-The-case-of-Spritz-1.pdf)), and readers silently substitute context-plausible words 26–40% of the time at 600 wpm **despite explicit verbatim instructions** ([Potter et al. 1993 via PSPI](http://faculty.cas.usf.edu/eschotter/papers/Rayner_Schotter_Masson_Potter_Treiman_2016_PSPI.pdf)). Concretely: **drop to ~200 wpm or exit RSVP entirely for tables, equations and numeric runs**, and use ~2× dwell as the starting multiplier for context-free tokens ([Primativo et al.](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0153786)) — a factor of two, not five.
- **Rejoin numeric and abbreviation tokens BEFORE classifying sentence boundaries.** Naive punctuation tokenisation splits `3.14` into two slides *and* fires a 450 ms "sentence end" on the decimal point. [Sprint Reader](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthonynosek/sprint-reader-chrome/master/src/engine.js) implements exactly this recursive rejoin; the [Spritz patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20140016867A1/en) excludes punctuation from the length bucket so `1,234,567.89` is not mis-bucketed. Both split long tokens (>13 and >17 chars respectively) with a hyphen — **suppress splitting for numeric strings**, where fragmentation is most dangerous.
- **Put the slack between sentences, never inside them.** Slowing within-sentence rate re-invites eye movements and hurt efficiency ([Rahman & Muter](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10354807/)); a punctuation pause is the one modification users actually liked ([Castelhano & Muter](https://web.archive.org/web/20071211155000/http://www.psych.utoronto.ca:80/users/muter/castelhanoandmuter.htm)) and is independently justified by measured sentence-final elevation (+48 ms gaze, **+121 ms regression-path**, [Payne & Stine-Morrow](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3376187/)) and by unmasking stream neighbours.
- **Expose the inter-word blank gap as a parameter and do not set it to zero by default.** A gap does not lower the decoding floor ([Rubin & Turano](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1604858/): 69.4 ms "was not reduced by increasing ISI"), but a blank interval after an attended item is the one manipulation shown to abolish the attentional blink ([Raymond et al. 1992](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.18.3.849) — letters, detection task, so **the transfer to word streams is UNVERIFIED**), and it unmasks stream neighbours ([Primativo et al.](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0153786)). Two mechanisms point the same way at essentially no cost; label the justification as mechanistic, not demonstrated.
- **Do not fold repeated tokens together, and consider marking a within-sentence repeat.** Repetition blindness drops the second occurrence of a repeated word ~24–30% of the time at 117 ms/word and produces ungrammatical output the reader does not notice ([Bond & Andrews 2008](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/MC.36.2.295.pdf)). Whether it persists at 200–350 ms/word is unknown, so this is an **unverified engineering judgement**: at defensible reading rates the cheapest insurance is the punctuation pause you are already inserting, plus not shortening dwell on a token identical to a recent one.
- **Cap per-word dwell around 300–350 ms.** Above that you are inside the 200–330 ms temporal-crowding window and past the point where readers start saccading around a static word ([Primativo et al.](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0153786); [Rahman & Muter](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10354807/)). A 2.2–2.75× length ramp on a 150 ms base breaches this.
- **Use a length multiplier of ~1.1–1.4× across the realistic length range, not 2×+.** Empirical target is 1.11× (gaze) to 1.36× (adjusted gaze) for 11 vs 5 letters ([Rayner et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3543826/)); the patent's 1.0/1.3/1.6 step function is the only shipped scheme that lands in range.
- **If you scale by frequency or surprisal, use ~2–4 ms/bit.** Cross-linguistic MECO estimate ([Wilcox et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.03667)); Sprint Reader's 12.3 ms/bit is 3–6× too steep and disables the user's speed control.
- **Do not shorten dwell for common function words.** Tested, disliked, suspected harmful, and the effect size does not support it anyway (Kliegl: −4.6 ms per decade of frequency, not 115 ms) ([Castelhano & Muter](https://web.archive.org/web/20071211155000/http://www.psych.utoronto.ca:80/users/muter/castelhanoandmuter.htm); [Kliegl et al.](https://d-nb.info/1218393807/34)).
- **Centre words; do not left-justify. Treat the coloured pivot as a UI affordance, not a comprehension feature.** ORP alignment plausibly beats left-justification ([Rayner et al. 2016](http://faculty.cas.usf.edu/eschotter/papers/Rayner_Schotter_Masson_Potter_Treiman_2016_PSPI.pdf)) but is ω²=0.00 against centring on comprehension, workload, alpha and theta ([Kosch et al.](https://thomaskosch.com/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/kosch2020one.pdf)). **If you implement ORP, copy the patent's `ceil((L−1)/4)` capped at 4 on punctuation-stripped words** — it is the only version with documented provenance (such as it is), and five of the nine shipped implementations surveyed invent their own rule instead.
- **Spend design budget on speed and boundary pauses, not the pivot.** Speed dominates every measured outcome (ω² = 0.08 / 0.27 / 0.61 / 0.58) while alignment moves nothing ([Kosch et al.](https://thomaskosch.com/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/kosch2020one.pdf)).
- **Provide a real regression affordance, and accept it will be inferior to eyes.** Regression removal alone costs 84% → 71%, globally ([Schotter et al. 2014](http://faculty.cas.usf.edu/eschotter/papers/Schotter_Tran_Rayner_2014_PsychSci.pdf)); the failure is silent because readers stop attempting repairs. Rewind-by-sentence with the surrounding text visible is closer to a spatial representation than restart-from-beginning.
- **Make rate per-user adaptive, or at least trivially adjustable mid-stream.** Perceptual span varies ~60% between fast and slow readers with comprehension equated ([Rayner, Slattery & Bélanger](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/PBR.17.6.834.pdf)), and Rayner et al. state devices "may need to be tailored to each individual user."
- **If you evaluate this feature, include a no-read control group.** Non-readers scored **53%** on the Di Nocera T/F instrument and **57%** on Carver's; without that baseline your comprehension number is mostly prior knowledge.
- **Consider ADHD readers as a first-class use case.** The one study where RSVP wins: **+6.96% for ADHD readers, −5.82% for neurotypical controls, at 250 ms/word + 50 ms gap (~200 wpm)** ([Moussaoui et al. 2025, N = 76](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/33851CEA7C1AC6193D088D4D5551ED3C/S1355617725101628a.pdf/div-class-title-reading-without-eye-movements-improving-reading-comprehension-in-young-adults-with-attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder-adhd-div.pdf)). *Rate corrected during audit: the paper specifies 0.25 s per word with no inter-word gap — **240 wpm**, not the 200 wpm stated in the first draft. It was an online study run on participants' own monitors, so per-word timing was refresh-rate dependent.* This is the strongest positive framing available for the feature, and it points at low rates, not high ones.
- **Frame RSVP honestly in-product: it fails at speed, not in principle.** At normal rates comprehension is comparable ([Hester et al. 2016](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1541931213601300); [PSPI's own concession](http://faculty.cas.usf.edu/eschotter/papers/Rayner_Schotter_Masson_Potter_Treiman_2016_PSPI.pdf)), and RSVP's genuine niche is time-boxed skimming and small displays — "If the message is not too long or complex, RSVP may suffice." **An academic PDF violates both qualifiers.**

## 8. Unresolved / unverified

- **How numerals, equations, tables and citation strings behave under RSVP.** Zero evidence in any retrieved source; three independent sweeps (including two of mine over OpenAlex) returned nothing on fixation durations for numerals in running text. The ~2× context-free-token multiplier from [Primativo et al.](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0153786) is a **proxy, not a measurement**. *Would settle it:* an in-house verbatim-recall study on numeric passages at 200/300/400 wpm with a no-read control; or a corpus study reporting fixation coefficients for Arabic numerals in prose.
- **Whether variable dwell improves comprehension at all.** [Castelhano & Muter](https://web.archive.org/web/20071211155000/http://www.psych.utoronto.ca:80/users/muter/castelhanoandmuter.htm) found a null (*F*(2,250)=2.53, *p*>.05) with a speed loss; nothing since compares variable vs constant dwell **at matched mean delivered rate**. The 2026 spaCy-based system reports only "preliminary evaluations." *Would settle it:* a within-subjects experiment at fixed effective wpm, comprehension probes separating literal from inferential.
- **Decomposition of the RSVP deficit into regression-loss vs preview-loss vs boundary-pause-loss.** [Schotter et al.](http://faculty.cas.usf.edu/eschotter/papers/Schotter_Tran_Rayner_2014_PsychSci.pdf) isolate regressions, [Masson 1983](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.3758/bf03196973) isolates boundary pauses, but **no study crosses these factors** — so how much a well-designed reader could recover with boundary pauses alone is unknown.
- **The dwell threshold at which a modern reader starts saccading around a static centred word.** [Rahman & Muter](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10354807/) inferred rather than eye-tracked this, in 1999, on CRTs, at ~260 wpm. This is the single most decision-relevant unknown for any scheme granting hard words 300–400 ms. *Would settle it:* an eye-tracked pilot at your actual font size and viewing distance.
- **The resume ramp.** UNVERIFIED — no experimental support found anywhere. The [Spritz patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20140016867A1/en) mentions an unquantified ramp; [speedread](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pasky/speedread/master/speedread) uses a 0.2 s first-word floor; [tspreed](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n-ivkovic/tspreed/master/tspreed) uses 1000 ms first-word / 500 ms post-unpause. Candidate mechanisms point opposite ways: attentional-blink refractoriness argues for slower resumption, while the unmasked-first-word advantage (1365 vs 496 wpm) argues the word after a gap is the *easiest* in the stream. Ship it as a tunable and label its justification as comfort, not comprehension.
- **Brysbaert & Nazir (2005).** UNVERIFIED — not retrieved (Academia.edu 403; search budget exhausted). The patent's claim that B&N supplied ORP positions only for lengths 3/5/7/9 rests entirely on the patent, an interested party. *Would settle it:* the B&N PDF via crr.ugent.be or a library proxy.
- **O'Regan et al. (1984) and the true OVP slope.** Not retrieved. [Rayner 1998](http://andrewd.ces.clemson.edu/courses/cpsc881/papers/reading/Ray98_readingSurvey.pdf) says 20 ms/letter; [Rayner et al. 2016](http://faculty.cas.usf.edu/eschotter/papers/Rayner_Schotter_Masson_Potter_Treiman_2016_PSPI.pdf) says a few ms, from the same citation. *Would settle it:* O'Regan et al. 1984 (*Vision Research* 24:917–928) and Vitu, O'Regan & Mittau (1990) in full.
- ~~**Schwalm, Radach & Kuperman (2026) numbers.**~~ **RESOLVED during audit.** The article is CC-BY and the [publisher PDF](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/10888438.2025.2612649) fetches cleanly with a browser user-agent. Verified: *Scientific Studies of Reading* 30(4):327–350, N = 46 native English-speaking students, speeds **225 / 270 / 350 / 360 / 405 wpm** set by line-by-line enhancement within a page ("45 wpm intervals"), narrative passages up to 619 words, **6 four-option comprehension questions per text (chance 25%)**. Comprehension by condition (baseline → 405): **.56 / .58 / .56 / .58 / .57 / .49**; total reading time 300 → 223 ms; regression-out probability falls but is never eliminated. **The new caveat this creates:** comprehension "held" at ~56% correct against a 25% floor, which is a weak absolute level, so the study establishes *no decline until 405 wpm on this instrument* rather than *good comprehension at 360 wpm*. The RSVP-vs-page contrast in §3 survives but is softer than the first draft implied.
- **Whether Rubin & Turano's 1652 wpm cell means anything.** Their Expt 2 reports 6 of 13 subjects at "the maximum testable rate" with ≥75% comprehension — a **censored** observation, with the comprehension instrument, chance level and passage type unrecoverable from the abstract and the full text paywalled with no OA copy found. *Would settle it:* the 1992 *Vision Research* full text, specifically the Expt 2 method and the distribution of the comprehension scores.
- **Whether repetition blindness survives at usable reading rates.** Demonstrated at 100–120 ms/word ([Bond & Andrews 2008](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/MC.36.2.295.pdf)); nothing retrieved tests 200–350 ms/word, and nothing tests it with a comprehension rather than a whole-report outcome. *Would settle it:* an SOA sweep (100/150/200/300 ms) with repeated content words in prose, scored for both report and comprehension.
- **Whether the attentional blink applies between successive words in prose.** [Raymond et al. 1992](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.18.3.849) is letters and a detection task; the blank-interval rescue is the most actionable timing result retrieved and the least directly applicable. *Would settle it:* a word-stream blink experiment, or an in-house A/B of gap = 0 vs gap = 30–50 ms at fixed delivered wpm.
- **Whether Kosch's ω²=0.00 means "no effect" or "underpowered".** N=17, no equivalence test, no CI on the alignment effect. The defensible statement is "no detectable effect on four outcomes in a design that detected large speed effects on the same measures", **not** "proven to do nothing". A small true effect (d≈0.2) could have been missed. *Would settle it:* a preregistered equivalence-tested replication, or a cheap in-house centred-vs-ORP test at your font size with a **literal-recall** measure — nobody has tested ORP on a display large enough that word letters exceed the 2° fovea.
- **Whether Acklin & Papesh's text difficulty interacted with speed.** The one direct test of "does harder text make RSVP worse"; the published abstract does not report the interaction and the full text is paywalled with no OA copy. *Would settle it:* institutional access to *Am. J. Psychology* 130(2):183–199, specifically the difficulty × speed term and the four cell means.
- **Whether any RSVP cost shrinks with practice.** *Amended during audit — the first draft's "no practice evidence" was too strong.* One retrieved dataset bears on it: [Dingler et al. AH'15](https://kaikunze.de/papers/pdf/dingler2015assessment.pdf) found RSVP comprehension rising **4.58 → 6.0 out of 10 across four rounds in a single session** at 150% of each reader's baseline wpm (*t*(11) = 2.75, *p* = .01, *r* = .64), with n = 12, no no-practice control, and no separation of practice from text order. Every other retrieved study used RSVP-naive participants with a single short practice text. Di Nocera et al. close with "It remains to be determined if, with additional practice and improved methods, good levels of reading comprehension at high rates can be achieved." **No *longitudinal* RSVP training study with comprehension outcomes exists in the retrieved literature**, and the one within-session signal is confounded and could be practice on the question format.
- **Modern replacement for the 1978 reference reader table.** Both canonical reviews still print [Rayner 1978](http://andrewd.ces.clemson.edu/courses/cpsc881/papers/reading/Ray98_readingSurvey.pdf) per-reader means (216 ms / 8.1 chars / 11% / 308 wpm). GECO and MECO give comparable per-word durations but I found no modern equivalent reporting reader-level saccade length, regression % and wpm together.
- **English (not German) ms-per-letter and ms-per-log-frequency slopes on gaze duration from a large naturalistic corpus.** [Kliegl et al.](https://d-nb.info/1218393807/34) is German isolated sentences; [Rayner et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3543826/) is English but only three length bins. Surprisal papers treat length/frequency as nuisance controls and do not print their coefficients. *Would settle it:* refitting Dundee/Provo/GECO directly.
- **Whether Spritz ever published or commissioned ORP research.** Only the 2015 archives were checked (deliberately, to match what the peer-reviewed critiques responded to). The company's current status was **not verified in this pass** (the first draft's "still existed as of 2024" is unsourced and should not be relied on). *Would settle it:* fetching spritz.com as of 2026.

### The defects fixed

1. **Selective citation of the strongest counter-evidence (severe).** [Rubin & Turano 1992](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1604858/) was cited *only* for its 69.4 ms floor. The same abstract reports **1171 wpm RSVP vs 303 wpm page** and **6 of 13 subjects at 1652 wpm with at least 75% comprehension**. A dossier arguing RSVP is oversold cannot quietly drop the one result that argues the other way. Added as its own subsection in section 3, with the four conditions that limit it and Schwalm et al.'s own verbatim dismissal of it.
2. **Wrong presentation rate** for Moussaoui et al. (200 corrected to 240 wpm), in sections 3 and 7.
3. **Overstated conflict.** Conflict (b) claimed Di Nocera's summary of Boo & Conklin was contradicted by the primary. It is not: the *inferential/gist* null at 500 wpm is real (*z*=-1.13, *p*=.26). Rewritten as what it actually is -- selective quotation of the one null in a table of losses -- with the countervailing point that Boo & Conklin's gist measure is a single item and should not be cited as a positive finding either.
4. **Unsupported patent arithmetic** ("12 of 18") corrected; the patent's unpublished 20-subject validation claim added and discounted.
5. **Implementation counts** ("four of ten / six of ten") did not match the dossier's own table; corrected to four of nine reproducing the patent rule, five inventing their own.
6. **Numbers without conditions:** added *n*=6 / oral reading / Italian to every Primativo figure including the 2x context-free multiplier; the 50% chance floor to Schotter; 25% chance and 56% absolute to Schwalm; three-option format and the single gist item to Boo & Conklin; Figure-1 provenance to Di Nocera's six percentages.

### Missing angles found and added

- **Repetition blindness.** Roughly 24-30% of repeated words are dropped from report at 117 ms/word, producing ungrammatical sentences the reader does not notice ([Bond & Andrews 2008](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/MC.36.2.295.pdf), N=22; [Kanwisher 1987](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0277(87)90016-3)). A second silent-failure mechanism alongside Potter's substitutions, and one that targets exactly the repeated technical terms academic prose is made of. Whether it survives at 200-350 ms/word is unknown.
- **The attentional blink.** A ~270 ms window opening ~180 ms after an attended item, **abolished by a blank interval after that item** ([Raymond, Shapiro & Arnell 1992](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.18.3.849)). This separates two things the dossier had run together: Rubin & Turano's "ISI does not lower the decoding floor" is not "gaps are useless." Produced a new design bullet.
- **The inverted OVP effect.** [Vitu, McConkie, Kerr & O'Regan 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11718792/), three corpora: in actual reading, **mean fixation duration is greatest, not lowest, at word centre**. From O'Regan's own lab -- the same source the entire ORP story is built on. This is stronger against the ORP mechanism than the Kosch null, because the null could be underpowered and this is a directional reversal in large corpora.
- **Practice data.** [Dingler et al.](https://kaikunze.de/papers/pdf/dingler2015assessment.pdf) do report RSVP comprehension rising 4.58 to 6.0 out of 10 over four rounds in one session (*t*(11)=2.75, *p*=.01) -- confounded and small, but enough that "no practice evidence exists" was too strong. Their RSVP eye tracking (118 fixations/text, mean fixation 1044 ms) independently corroborates Benedetto.

### What remains genuinely unresolved after this pass

The section 8 list stands, minus Schwalm (resolved), plus three new entries (Rubin & Turano's censored 1652 wpm cell; whether repetition blindness survives at usable rates; whether the attentional blink applies word-to-word in prose). Beyond those, the three that would change design decisions most:

1. **Numerals, equations, tables and citation strings under RSVP.** Still zero direct evidence after a fourth independent sweep. The best proxy is a 2x multiplier from *n*=6 Italians reading random word lists aloud with no comprehension measure. For a numeric-fidelity PDF reader this is the central unknown and it is unaddressed by the entire literature.
2. **Whether variable dwell helps comprehension at matched delivered rate.** [Castelhano & Muter 2001](https://web.archive.org/web/20071211155000/http://www.psych.utoronto.ca:80/users/muter/castelhanoandmuter.htm) is still the only direct test, it is null with a speed loss, and it is 25 years old. Every shipped timing scheme in section 6 is therefore uncalibrated.
3. **The dwell ceiling at which a modern reader starts saccading around a static centred word.** [Rahman & Muter 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10354807/) inferred it rather than measuring it, on CRTs, at ~260 wpm. Every length- and difficulty-based dwell extension in section 7 is bounded by a number nobody has actually measured, and Dingler's 1044 ms mean RSVP fixation duration suggests the eyes are doing more during a static word than the design assumes.
