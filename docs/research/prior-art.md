# RSVP Prior Art: What to Steal, What They Got Wrong

**Bottom line**

1. The pivot rule is not proprietary. Spritz's own granted patent [US8903174B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8903174B2/en) publishes TABLE I in full — five buckets on character count, no linguistic analysis anywhere. Three independent implementations reproduce it exactly.
2. The best-evidenced experiment on the whole premise is negative. [Benedetto et al. 2015](https://www.tsw.it/wp-content/uploads/Rapid-serial-visual-presentation-in-reading-The-case-of-Spritz-1.pdf) (N=60, French novel, nominal 250 wpm, between-subjects, eye-tracked) measured **209 wpm vs 200 wpm for traditional reading (p=.160 — no speed gain at all)** while literal comprehension fell from 72% to 60% (p=.009). Caveat that matters and that the paper states outright: the stimulus was a Matlab reimplementation of the Spritz *patent*, not the shipped SDK. So this indicts the published method, not the product. Across this and [Acklin & Papesh 2017](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:29461715&resultType=core&format=json) the replicated pattern is directional: **RSVP costs literal/verbatim detail and leaves inferential gist intact.** You do not get speed for free; you may not get speed at all.
3. **No shipping RSVP product preserves numeric fidelity through its tokenizer.** Squirt splits `3.14` into two slides, Sprint Reader merges `Table 2, 15` into `2,15`, zotero-rsvp deletes `±` and `=`. For a numbers-critical reader this is the disqualifying class of bug.
4. Zero column detection exists anywhere. Every PDF-capable RSVP reader reads two-column papers in content-stream order.
5. Timing: steal Spritz's **duration normalization** (divide the WPM budget by mean word relative duration so nominal WPM is honoured) — patented, described, and copied by nobody.
6. Rendering: steal speeedy's `grid-template-columns: 1fr auto 1fr`. Zero measurement, zero forced layout, proportional fonts.
7. Scheduling: everyone uses chained `setTimeout` with no drift correction and no `visibilitychange` pause. At 800 WPM the real noise floor is display refresh quantization (±11% on 60 Hz), not timer jitter.
7a. Accessibility is a universal blind spot with a one-line fix. RSVP is auto-updating text that starts automatically and runs longer than five seconds, which puts it squarely inside [WCAG 2.2 SC 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html) — a **Level A** criterion — and no surveyed project responds to [`prefers-reduced-motion`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-motion). See §7.
8. Architecture lesson: Spritz put tokenization behind an API and the algorithm died with the host (`api.spritzinc.com` no longer resolves). jetzt's self-contained 16 KB bookmarklet still runs nine years after its last commit.

## 2. Per-project detail

### Spritz (proprietary, dead service)

**Ingestion:** cloud. The SDK bundle contains exactly one API base, `https://api.spritzinc.com/api-server/v1/`, with OAuth, and its word model is `function f(a,b,c,d,e){ this.word=a, this.orp=b, this.multiplier=c, this.position=d, this.flags=e }` — the ORP index and timing multiplier arrive **per word from the server**, per the [SDK source](https://sdk.spritzinc.com/js/2.0.16/js/spritz.min.js). Downstream confirmation that this killed it: the still-maintained ReadFast extension shipped ["Replace the spritz SDK that is no longer available by a custom OPR algorithm"](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/readfast-powered-by-sprit/blbpafkooanpdcdcndkcckblghjddpke).

**Chunking:** the [patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8903174B2/en) specifies splitting words >13 characters into two elements joined by a hyphen. The shipped SDK does not split at render time — it shrinks the font in a measure loop, `do { b--; j.font = b+"px "+m } while (j.measureText(a).width > c)` — implying splitting was server-side.

**Timing:** patent Fig. 5a sets length multipliers ≤7 chars ×1.0, 8–13 ×1.3, >13 ×1.6. Fig. 5b is the idea nobody copied: the blank element inserted between sentences scales by the length **in words of the sentence just read** — ≤7 words ×1.0, 8–22 ×2.2, >22 ×3.3. Working-memory load, not punctuation. The SDK's UI constants: bookmarklet slider 5–1000 wpm step 5, default 250 ([Spritzlet](https://sdk.spritzinc.com/bookmarklet/latest/html/spritzlet.html)); resume countdown `z <= 400 ? 750 : z >= 800 ? 350 : 1150 - z` ms.

**Rendering:** HTML5 canvas. Prefix width `l = round(measure(word[0:orp]))`, half-pivot `n = round(measure(word[orp])/2)`, painted at `x = U - l - n` / `U - n` / `U + n` where `U` is the reticle x.

### jetzt (Apache-2.0, frozen 2017)

**Ingestion:** 100% local, zero network. `select.js` uses `window.getSelection()` or a hover picker; `parse.dom()` walks childNodes, taking `textContent` when every child is `display:inline`, else dispatching on nodeName (H1–H6 → heading wrap, BLOCKQUOTE → `›`, P/UL/OL/DL/SECTION → paragraph modifiers), per [parse.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ds300/jetzt/master/modules/parse.js) and [select.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ds300/jetzt/master/modules/select.js).

**Chunking:** one regex in [parse.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ds300/jetzt/master/modules/parse.js) — `text.match(/["«»“”\(\)\/–—]|--+|\n+|[^\s"“«»”\(\)\/–—]+/g)` — treating quotes, parens, slashes and dashes as structural tokens that push/pop a nested "wrap" stack rendered as side-panel chrome. Long words split when `word.length > 13 || word.length > 9 && word.indexOf("-") > -1`, into `Math.ceil(word.length / 8)` blind slices.

**Timing (exact, from [exec.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ds300/jetzt/master/modules/exec.js) and [config.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ds300/jetzt/master/modules/config.js)):** base `60*1000/target_wpm`. Length multipliers by `switch(len)`: 6–7 →1.2, 8–9 →1.4, 10–11 →1.8, 12–13 →2. Modifier multipliers: normal 1, start_clause 1, end_clause 1.8, start_sentence 1.3, end_sentence 2.2, start_paragraph 2.0, end_paragraph 2.8, short_space 1.5, long_space 2.2. Defaults: `target_wpm` 400, clamped `100…1500`, arrows ±10 wpm. **Critical structure:** the modifier branch returns early, so length and modifier never multiply — a 13-char sentence-final word gets 2.2×, not 4.4×.

**Rendering:** three DOM spans in an absolutely positioned `.sr-word`, with a forced reflow and read-back every word (see §3).

### OpenSpritz (MIT, two divergent forks)

**Ingestion:** the selection path uses `document.URL` → `https://api.diffbot.com/v2/article?...&token=2efef432c72b5a923408e04353c39a7c`, a hardcoded credential live in a 1564★ repo and [still functional in 2026](https://api.diffbot.com/v2/article?url=https%3A%2F%2Fexample.com&token=2efef432c72b5a923408e04353c39a7c).

**Timing:** there is none ([spritz.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Miserlou/Glance-Bookmarklet/master/spritz.js)). `var ms_per_word = 60000/wpm` drives a single fixed `setInterval`; "pauses" are faked in a preprocessing pass that splices duplicate word tokens (3 frames) and blank `' '` tokens into the array. Pauses are quantized to whole multiples of the base interval and the word visibly *repeats* rather than lingering.

**Rendering:** no measurement at all — pad with `.` characters to a fixed 22-character field, hide them with `.invisible { color: #ffffff }`, and rely on `'Droid Sans Mono'`.

### Sprint Reader (BSD-3, MV3, maintained)

**Ingestion:** selection-only, `content_scripts` matching `["http://*/*", "https://*/*"]` — **no `file://`, no PDF path at all.** A [2014 review by an academic user](https://software4scholars.wordpress.com/2014/04/19/speed-reading-pdfs-using-the-spritz-technique/), testing Sprint Reader by name on journal PDFs, is the most concrete failure report in this dossier and is worth quoting rather than paraphrasing. On indiscriminate ingestion: the reader *"will – not surprisingly – read all the text in the pdf, which means it will also read the header on each page, the page numbers, and – most annoyingly, the text inserted on every page by various publishers."* The worked example is the JSTOR stamp — `"This content downloaded from xxx.xxx.16.16 on Thu, 17 Apr 2014 06:49:43 AM All use subject to JSTOR Terms and Conditions"` — injected into the word stream once per page. On the context menu, an UPDATE appended to the same post: *"Sprint Reader sometimes seems to have problems rendering text in pdfs…when I select and right click other parts, there is not even a possibility to select 'Sprint read selected text'."* Twelve years old and, per the source-reading in §7, still unfixed. Note this is a user blog, cited as the primary record of that user's own testing, not as an independent evaluation.

**Timing:** three algorithms in [engine.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthonynosek/sprint-reader-chrome/master/src/engine.js). The interesting one is information-theoretic: `bitsOfInformation = -Math.log2(wordProbability[t.text])` against a SUBTLEX-US subtitle corpus, linearly interpolated between empirical endpoints 4.5 bits → 40 ms and 25.6 bits → 300 ms (1500 → 200 WPM, a 7.5× range), floored at 40 ms. Unknown words pin to the 300 ms maximum. The in-source comment is candid: *"Note: this means WPM setting has no role in this algorithm."* Punctuation delays are **absolute ms, not multipliers** — comma 250, period 450, paragraph 700 — so at 400 WPM a period costs 3 word-times and at 1800 WPM it costs 13.6.

### speeedy (MIT, active, closest to our shape)

**Ingestion:** [text-parser.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sami-29/speeedy/main/src/services/text-parser.ts) uses pdfjs-dist with the worker imported as a bundled `?url` asset — correctly local-first. Line reconstruction from `item.transform`: new line when `|y - lastY| > fontSize * 0.5`, space when `x - lastX > fontSize * 0.3` with `lastX = x + str.length * fontSize * 0.5` (a fixed 0.5-em advance estimate — wrong for proportional academic fonts in both directions). No column detection, no de-hyphenation.

**Timing** ([rsvp-engine.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sami-29/speeedy/main/src/services/rsvp-engine.ts))**:** `msPerWord = 60000/finalWpm`; `delay = msPerWord * chunkLength * (1 + (pauseMultiplier - 1) * sentencePauseMultiplier)`, then `* paragraphPauseMultiplier` on paragraph start and `* 1.15` on a closing aside. Pause multipliers `.!?`→2.0, `,;`→1.4, `:`→1.4. Defaults wpm 300, sentencePause 2, paragraphPause 1.8, rewindStep 5.

### pasky/speedread (MIT, the reference model)

[speedread](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pasky/speedread/master/speedread) reads stdin, so ingestion is somebody else's problem (`pdftotext file.pdf - | speedread -w 400`) — the most durable architecture in the survey. Constants: `$wordtime=0.9, $lentime=0.04, $commatime=2, $fstoptime=3, $multitime=1.2, $firsttime=0.2` seconds. The formula is **base replacement, then additive length, then scale**: `$time = $wordtime`; replaced by `$fstoptime` on `/[.?!]\W*$/` or `$commatime` on `/[:;,]\W*$/`; then `$time += sqrt(length($word)) * $lentime`; then `$time *= 60/$wpm`. At 250 wpm a plain 5-letter word is 237 ms and a sentence-final one 741 ms.

## 3. Rendering: how the pivot is held stationary

Five genuinely distinct techniques exist. Ranked by cost-to-correctness:

| Technique | Project | Per-word cost | Proportional fonts? | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CSS 3-column grid** | [speeedy](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sami-29/speeedy/HEAD/src/index.css) | **zero** | yes | `grid-template-columns: 1fr auto 1fr`, side tracks `min-width:0`, pivot `flex-shrink:0` |
| Terminal column arithmetic | [tspreed](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n-ivkovic/tspreed/master/tspreed) | zero | monospace only | `word_x=$((term_x_center - word_focus_pos + 1))` |
| Prerender-and-measure one beat early | [Squirt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cameron/squirt/master/squirt.js) | off critical path | yes | measures next word inside `.word-prerenderer { visibility:hidden }` |
| Canvas `measureText`, precomputed at parse | [Sprint Reader](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthonynosek/sprint-reader-chrome/HEAD/src/engine.js), [splashreader](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rattrayalex/splashreader/master/src/lib/rsvp.js) | zero at playback | yes, with caveats | `measureText(prefix) + measureText(pivot)/2` |
| Three spans + forced sync layout | [jetzt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ds300/jetzt/master/modules/view.js) | 1 forced reflow/word | yes | `word.offsetWidth;` then read `pivotChar.offsetLeft` |
| Invisible monospace padding | [OpenSpritz](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pirate/OpenSpritz/master/spritz.js) | zero | **no** | pad to 22 chars, `.invisible { color:#ffffff }` |

**Recommendation: the CSS grid.** It is the only technique that is simultaneously free, font-agnostic, and immune to the measurement/shaping mismatch below. Squirt's prerenderer is the right fallback if you need measured placement — note `visibility:hidden` is load-bearing, since [`offsetParent` returns null under `display:none`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/HTMLElement/offsetParent).

**Canvas measurement is subtly wrong in both implementations that use it.** Sprint Reader's canvas font string carries only size and family — no weight, no style, no letter-spacing — while it renders through jQuery Lettering, which wraps every glyph in its own span and thereby suppresses kerning across span boundaries. `measureText(prefix)` measured the prefix as one shaped run *including* internal kerning; the DOM renders it as isolated glyph advances. [MDN defines `TextMetrics.width`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/TextMetrics) only as the width of a segment "taking into account the current font of the context" — shaping behaviour is not specified, so this is not resolvable from spec. splashreader gets half of it right by feeding the *identical CSS font shorthand string* to both canvas and DOM, but still splits into three spans.

**Two shipping readers do not pivot-align at all.** [zotero-rsvp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Amogh-Joshi/zotero-rsvp/HEAD/content/rsvp.js) — the one built specifically for academic PDFs — uses `#zrsvp-word-area { justify-content:center }` and merely colours the ORP letter orange. The word is centred as a unit, so the pivot glyph's screen x moves with every word; monospace only quantizes that movement to whole cells. [HotGato](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TheRedJalapeno/HotGato_SpeedReader/HEAD/core.js), self-described as "the most advanced Speed Reader for RSVP on the internet", ends `nextChunk()` with `textOutput.textContent = chunkText`.

**A SwiftUI hazard worth knowing:** [khlebobul](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/khlebobul/speed-reader/HEAD/SpeedReader/SpeedReader/Views/Reader/ORPWordView.swift) measures via `GeometryReader` → `PreferenceKey` → `@State`, so on the frame a new word first renders the widths still hold the *previous* word's values. The word is positioned wrong for one frame then snaps — 11–25% of a word's display life at 800 WPM.

### ORP tables actually disagree

| Implementation | Rule (0-indexed pivot) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Spritz patent TABLE I | 1→0; 2–5→1; 6–9→2; 10–13→3; 14–20→4 | [US8903174B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8903174B2/en) |
| jetzt, splashreader, tspreed | same as patent | [jetzt view.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ds300/jetzt/master/modules/view.js) |
| **speedread** — *corrected by orchestrator 2026-08-29* | **proportional, not tabular:** `$ORPloc = 0.35; $ORPmax = 0.2;` fed to `find_ORP($word, $ORPloc)` | [speedread L57–58, L93, re-fetched and verified](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pasky/speedread/master/speedread) |
| Sprint Reader | 1→0; 2–4→1; 5–9→2; else 3 | [engine.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthonynosek/sprint-reader-chrome/HEAD/src/engine.js) |
| speeedy, quickreader | ≤1→0; ≤5/≤6→1; ≤9→2; else 3 | [rsvp-engine.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sami-29/speeedy/HEAD/src/services/rsvp-engine.ts) |
| Squirt | ≤1→0; 2–3→1; else `floor(len/2)-1` — **uncapped** | [squirt.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cameron/squirt/master/squirt.js) |
| OpenSpritz (pirate fork) | <6: split at 11 of 22; ≥6: always 4th char | [spritz.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pirate/OpenSpritz/master/spritz.js) |
| khlebobul | 13–17→4; 18→5; 19–25→`len/2`; >25→`0.6·len` | [ORPWordView.swift](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/khlebobul/speed-reader/HEAD/SpeedReader/SpeedReader/Views/Reader/ORPWordView.swift) |

Consensus against the [patent table](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8903174B2/en) is "index 3 or 4 for anything ≥10 characters", and the caps exist so the left-hand reserve stays bounded. Squirt's linear growth means a 20-char word pivots at index 9, requiring an unbounded left gutter.

One free win: [jetzt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ds300/jetzt/master/modules/view.js) strips trailing punctuation before bucketing — `calculatePivot(token.replace(/[?.,!:;*-]+$/, ""))` — so `therefore,` bucketing as 9 chars rather than 10. Spritz and OpenSpritz do not, so the red letter visibly jitters at sentence ends. speeedy goes further, computing on `coreText.replace(/[^\p{L}\p{N}]/gu, "")` and extending the pivot forward over combining marks so the highlight is a whole grapheme cluster.

### Conflicts

**The two "OpenSpritz" repos are materially different programs, and both researchers who reported contradictory failure modes were correct.** I fetched both files to settle this. [pirate/OpenSpritz](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pirate/OpenSpritz/master/spritz.js) has an unguarded polyfill `String.prototype.repeat = function(num){ return new Array(num+1).join(this) }` and computes `var tail = 22 - (word.length + 7)`; at length 17 that is `new Array(-1)` → **RangeError**, killing that one word's timer callback and freezing the previous word on screen. [Miserlou/Glance-Bookmarklet](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Miserlou/Glance-Bookmarklet/master/spritz.js) has a guarded polyfill `if(num < 1){ return new Array(Math.abs(num)+1).join(this) }` and the patent's 5-bucket switch; it does **not** throw — instead it emits `abs(n)` extra dots and the pivot drifts left by (length − 16) character widths. Different ORP rule, different failure. Cite the fork, never "OpenSpritz".

## 4. Scheduling and timer accuracy at high WPM

**Every implementation surveyed drives cadence from a wall clock.** Eight use chained `setTimeout` ([jetzt exec.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ds300/jetzt/master/modules/exec.js), [squirt.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cameron/squirt/master/squirt.js), [speeedy](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sami-29/speeedy/main/src/services/rsvp-engine.ts)) (jetzt, Squirt, speeedy, quickreader, HotGato, splashreader, Sprint Reader, thomaskolmans), one uses `setInterval` (zotero-rsvp), one uses `Timer.scheduledTimer` (khlebobul), one pre-schedules all N timers at absolute offsets (OpenSpritz), one uses shell `sleep` (tspreed). **None uses `requestAnimationFrame`, an audio clock, or an absolute-timeline accumulator.**

Chained `setTimeout` is structurally late-biased: each hop re-arms from the callback's own start, so lateness accumulates rather than cancelling. The [HTML spec](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/timers-and-user-prompts.html) states plainly that it "does not guarantee that timers will run exactly on schedule"; [web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/audio-scheduling) puts the per-hop magnitude at "tens of milliseconds or more". At 800 WPM the entire word budget is 75 ms.

**The only drift compensation anywhere is four lines of shell in [tspreed](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n-ivkovic/tspreed/master/tspreed)** — `sleep_ms_float + word_start_date - $(get_date_ms)` — and it is gated on `non_posix_date_n`, detected via `date "+%N"`. BSD `date` on macOS does not implement `%N`, so on a MacBook the compensation silently turns off and `--key-controls` hard-fails.

**Background throttling is an unrecognized ecosystem-wide bug.** Grepping for `visibilitychange|document.hidden|visibilityState` returns zero hits in jetzt, Squirt, HotGato, zotero-rsvp and quickreader; speeedy has a handler that only persists progress and does not pause. Alt-tab away from any of them and the reader silently burns position while you are not looking.

The mechanism, stated precisely from the primary source. Chrome's [intensive throttling](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/timer-throttling-in-chrome-88) (shipped Chrome 88, January 2021) reduces timer wake-ups to **once per minute** only when **all four** of these hold: the page has been hidden for more than 5 minutes; the chain count is 5 or greater; the page has been silent for at least 30 seconds; and WebRTC is not in use. An earlier draft of this dossier cited only the chain-count condition, which made the trap sound far more immediate than it is — a chained-`setTimeout` reader trips the chain-count condition on its fifth word, but that alone throttles nothing.

**The exact current grace period is UNVERIFIED and the previously stated "10 seconds" does not survive checking.** The [Intent to Ship for quick intensive throttling](https://groups.google.com/a/chromium.org/g/blink-dev/c/5SZB2CFFGqE) did propose cutting the hidden threshold from 5 minutes to 10 seconds targeting Chrome 109 — but reading the thread through, the API owners pushed back on compatibility grounds and asked for a compromise: *"If 1 minute achieves your goal of battery savings, could we go with that?"* The thread as served is truncated before the final resolution, so what actually shipped is not established by that source. Checking [Chromium's `blink/common/features.cc`](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/refs/heads/main/third_party/blink/common/features.cc) instead: `kIntensiveWakeUpThrottling` is `FEATURE_ENABLED_BY_DEFAULT` with a `grace_period_seconds` parameter documented as "the grace period during which there is no intensive wake up throttling after a page is hidden", still defaulting to 5 minutes. Conclusion: somewhere between 10 s and 5 min, field-trial configurable, and you should not design against a specific number.

**None of that changes what you should build, which is why the uncertainty is tolerable.** The fix is the same at any threshold — pause on `visibilitychange` — and it is strictly better than surviving throttling, because a reader that keeps advancing through a hidden tab is losing the user's place whether it does so at 800 WPM or at 1 WPM. Two implementation notes from the primary sources: a timer set from the `visibilitychange` handler itself is exempt from throttling (per the Intent to Ship: *"A timer set from the visibilitychange event handler, for example, won't be affected"*), and the audio-silence condition means a page playing audio is never intensively throttled — a tempting hack, and the wrong one, since it fixes the timer and not the fact that nobody is watching.

**The real noise floor is display quantization, not timer jitter.** [MDN notes](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Window/requestAnimationFrame) that rAF callbacks "generally match the display refresh rate", and a word cannot appear or vanish between frames. At 800 WPM the 75 ms budget is 4.5 frames on 60 Hz, so consecutive words alternate 66.7 / 83.3 ms — **±11% regardless of timer quality**. Frame-exact WPM values on 60 Hz are `3600/k`: 300, 400, 450, 600, 720, 900. On a 120 Hz ProMotion panel 75 ms is exactly 9 frames, so 800 WPM is clean there and dirty on an attached 60 Hz monitor. *(This arithmetic is derived from the cited refresh-rate statement, not from a retrieved measurement — see §8.)* Meanwhile clock resolution is irrelevant: [`performance.now()`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Performance/now) is coarsened to 5 µs isolated / 100 µs non-isolated, three orders of magnitude below a word.

**Mid-stream speed change has a stall bug in two implementations.** [speeedy](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sami-29/speeedy/HEAD/src/services/rsvp-engine.ts)'s `setWpm` does `clearTimer(); scheduleNext(settings, true)` and the Swift app does `timer?.invalidate()` plus a fresh `Timer` — both restart a *full* new delay from now, discarding elapsed time. A held arrow key repeating at 20–30 Hz resets the timer faster than the 75 ms word budget, freezing the display indefinitely. jetzt avoids this by never touching the in-flight timeout, at the cost of one word of latency.

**Rewind granularity:** [jetzt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ds300/jetzt/master/modules/exec.js)'s semantic rewind is the only one that lands somewhere meaningful — `index = Math.max(0, index - 5); while (index > 0 && !startModifiers[instructions[index].modifier]) index--;` — so tapping back within the first 5 words of a sentence jumps to the previous sentence, otherwise it restarts the current one. [Squirt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cameron/squirt/master/squirt.js)'s time-based rewind is broken twice over: it converts seconds to words using only the *base* interval, ignoring every multiplier (mean well above 1), and its "walk back to sentence start" loop has condition `nodeIdx < 0`, which can never be true.

## 6. What to steal

1. **Duration normalization — the single highest-value item.** Spritz divides the WPM budget by the summed relative duration of the passage: `computeStandardDelay: function(a){ var b = Math.round(this.rawWordCount/a*60*1e3); return Math.round(b/this.duration) }` ([SDK](https://sdk.spritzinc.com/js/2.0.16/js/spritz.min.js); described at patent step 718). Setting 400 wpm then actually yields 400 wpm averaged over the text. jetzt and OpenSpritz both multiply the base interval directly, so both run materially slower than their displayed target — jetzt's own `finishedReading()` wall-clock report will not match its setting.
2. **CSS `grid-template-columns: 1fr auto 1fr`** for pivot anchoring ([speeedy](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sami-29/speeedy/HEAD/src/index.css)). Zero forced layouts, proportional fonts, and a `translateX(%)` knob to move the fixation off dead-centre.
3. **Character-offset-preserving tokens.** Reedy carries `token.startIndex`/`endIndex` into the original raw string through the whole pipeline, which is what makes `getContext()`, `toProgress()` and click-to-seek all fall out for free ([Sequencer.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/olegcherr/Reedy-for-Chrome/master/js/content/Sequencer.js)).
4. **The `PauseableBlock` placeholder-beat model** ([khlebobul](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/khlebobul/speed-reader/main/SpeedReader/SpeedReader/Reader/RSVPEngine.swift)): non-linear content becomes one beat in the word index that halts playback and renders whole. Keyed by index, not string-matched. On resume it advances *past* the placeholder so Space feels instant. Independently reinvented by [speedread-skill](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jordan-gibbs/speedread-skill/main/README.md) and independently wished-for by the ReadKinetic author.
5. **Semantic rewind with 5-word back-off** ([jetzt exec.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ds300/jetzt/master/modules/exec.js)) — the correct affordance for "wait, what was that?".
6. **Sentence-length-scaled inter-sentence pause** (patent Fig. 5b: ≤7 words ×1.0, 8–22 ×2.2, >22 ×3.3). Scales with working-memory load rather than punctuation mark. Nobody copied it; it is in an expired-by-2033 but *published* disclosure.
7. **Split-word connector hyphen.** Sprint Reader appends `-` to every continued fragment and flags `childofprevious = true`, and re-glues a final fragment ≤4 chars. jetzt and Reedy both drop the connector, so the reader sees two apparent words.
8. **Page-offset seek** (zotero-rsvp's `pageOffsets[]`) — read the architecture, do not copy the code (PolyForm Noncommercial).
9. **Stdin/hook-based ingestion** (speedread, [ogma](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/0hAodha/ogma/main/README.md)). The ReadKinetic author's regret is instructive: bundling pdf.js, JSZip and mammoth into the download "and still could not handle formats that I had not seen."
10. **Grapheme-aware pivot indexing** via `Intl.Segmenter` ([quickreader](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/coinstax/quickreader/HEAD/src/lib/utils/orp.ts), speeedy). Only 2 of 10 do this; the rest index UTF-16 code units and will split surrogate pairs — exactly the math symbols and combining diacritics academic text is full of.

## 7. What they got wrong

### Numeric fidelity — the disqualifying class

| Project | Corruption | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Squirt | `3.14` → slides `3.` / `14`; `1,234` → `1,` / `234` | `text.replace(/[\,\.\!\:\;](?![\"\'\)\]\}])/g, "$& ")` before split, [squirt.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cameron/squirt/master/squirt.js) |
| Sprint Reader | `Table 2, 15 of 20` → one slide `2,15`; `91.4%, 3.2` → `91.4%,3.2` | `recursiveCheckForNumber` concatenates with **no separator**; default ON, [engine.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthonynosek/sprint-reader-chrome/master/src/engine.js) |
| OpenSpritz (Miserlou) | `3.14159` → `3•14159`; whole-page mode → two frames `3•` then `14159` | first `.` in any token → `&#8226;`; `text_content.replace(/\./g, '. ')` |
| zotero-rsvp | `x = 3.14 ± 0.02` → `x`, `3.14`, `0.02` — operators **deleted**, operands kept | `filter(w => /[a-zA-Z0-9]/.test(w))` |
| chislerr | any all-digit line deleted — table cells, equation numbers | `re.sub(r'\n\d+\n', '\n', text)` |
| speedread | `3-5`, `COVID-19`, `p-value` → two slides each | `split /(?:-|\s)+/` |
| jetzt | `3/4` → 3 frames; `doi:10.1038/nature12373` → 3 tokens | `/` is a structural token |

Only [Reedy](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/olegcherr/Reedy-for-Chrome/master/js/content/Parser.js) is defensible: all-digit tokens get the full `+1.1` complexity bonus (extra dwell) and are not restructured. Note the shared property of the worst cases — the output *looks plausible*. A reader at 600 wpm shown `2,15` has no signal that it is wrong.

### Structure

- **Zero column detection, universally.** thomaskolmans joins `items.map(i => i.str).join(' ')` in raw stream order; speeedy groups by y-delta but never partitions by x; chislerr calls PyMuPDF `get_text("text")` with no `sort=True`. The [2014 scholars' review](https://software4scholars.wordpress.com/2014/04/19/speed-reading-pdfs-using-the-spritz-technique/) named the running-header problem; twelve years later nobody has fixed it.
- **De-hyphenation is absent or unconditional, never correct.** speeedy does nothing (`hy-` / `phenated` as two slides); chislerr welds unconditionally (`well-known` → `wellknown`); Sprint Reader's `joinHyphenatedWord()` references an undefined global and does `replace("-/n", ...)` with a forward slash — it cannot work as written, yet defaults to true.
- **No concept of tables, captions, equations or superscripts** in the Spritz lineage. jetzt's `parse.dom` switch handles H1–H6, BLOCKQUOTE, SCRIPT, P/DL/OL/UL/SECTION and nothing else — TABLE, SUP, CODE, MATH fall to `default` and flatten into the word stream. TABLE is in neither `validParents` nor `validChildren`, so a table cannot even be *selected*.
- The ReadKinetic author's verdict on the naive approach: *"Extracting text flattens a table into a series of values in reading order, which is **worse than useless**."* And on captions: *"you see 'Figure 3 shows the relationship between' appearing one word at a time with no figure in sight."* ([HN](https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/items/49002344))
- **khlebobul's PDF table handling is deletion, not rendering.** It detects tables geometrically then emits `.table(html: "", columnCount: 0)`, and the empty html causes downstream filters to skip it — so on a PDF you get neither a table render nor a pause, just a silently removed table. Safe for digits; lossy for content.

### Platform and architecture

- **MV2 is over.** Chrome's [official timeline](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/migrate/mv2-deprecation-timeline): disabling began in stable 2024-10-09; default-disabled all channels 2025-03-31; **from Chrome 138 on 2025-07-24 users "can no longer turn them back on"**; and **all remaining MV2 extensions are removed from the Chrome Web Store on 2026-08-31 — two days from this writing.** This kills jetzt's and Reedy's extension paths outright.
- **"Local-first" that fetches from a CDN isn't.** thomaskolmans sets `workerSrc` to a runtime `https://unpkg.com/...` URL; [issue #12](https://github.com/thomaskolmans/rsvp-reading/issues/12) (Feb 2026, still open) reports `Setting up fake worker failed` both locally and on the demo.
- **Live bugs in frozen master:** jetzt's `configBackend.get` has an inverted test (`if(json) { cb("{}") } else { cb(json) }`), so saved options are never restored; and the recursive setter calls an undefined `_put`, so `jetzt.config(['modifiers','end_sentence'], 2.5)` — the documented retuning path — throws.
- **Scanned PDFs fail silently everywhere except two projects.** khlebobul detects <20% valid character bounds and routes to Apple Vision OCR; [rsvpnano's spec](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ionutdecebal/rsvpnano/main/docs/conversion/spec.md) mandates that "A PDF with no decodable text must fail with a clear error instead of producing an empty book" and that a converter "must reject undecodable text rather than emit character codes as text". That last case — a font with no `ToUnicode` map — is the nastiest, because it produces plausible-looking garbage rather than an error.
- **E-ink is refresh-bound.** KOReader's `DEFAULT_FULL_REFRESH_COUNT = 6` promotes every sixth partial refresh to a flashing full refresh; at the plugin's advertised 400 WPM that is a full-screen flash roughly every 0.9 s. The ReadKinetic author's quantified read, [verified verbatim during audit](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49002344): *"Partial refresh allows for a handful of updates each second, but it comes with ghosting… Full refresh clears that, but it flashes the entire panel and takes long enough that you would only read about a word per second."* That is a hard ceiling of roughly **60 WPM** for artefact-free e-ink RSVP — below normal silent reading, which makes the format pointless on that hardware. (An earlier draft rendered the ghosting half of this quote as "reading through the residue of the last two or three words"; that phrasing does not appear in the thread and has been replaced with the actual text. The "two or three words" figure is unsourced — dropped.) Corroborating by revealed preference: rsvpnano, a dedicated 1247★ RSVP *device*, uses LCD and AMOLED — not e-paper.

### Accessibility — the angle nobody in this survey addresses

Added during audit. Not one of the eighteen projects surveyed mentions accessibility conformance, and RSVP is an unusually exposed format: it is text that moves, cannot be re-read without an explicit control, and removes the regressions that struggling readers rely on most.

- **RSVP is squarely inside WCAG 2.2 Success Criterion 2.2.2 "Pause, Stop, Hide", at conformance Level A** — the lowest bar, the one nothing is allowed to fail. The criterion covers "moving, blinking, scrolling, or auto-updating information", where auto-updating is defined as [content that "updates or disappears based on a preset time interval"](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html) and the listed examples include "auto-advancing presentations and messages". The trigger conditions are content that starts automatically, lasts more than five seconds, and is presented in parallel with other content. An RSVP stream meets the first two by construction. The saving grace is that most readers here happen to satisfy it — a spacebar pause is the universal convention — but they satisfy it by accident, and the two that matter are the ones that autoplay on invocation without a visible (not just keyboard) pause affordance. If the reader is a panel alongside the page rather than a takeover, the "in parallel with other content" condition is met too and this becomes non-negotiable.
- **Zero projects honour [`prefers-reduced-motion`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-motion).** Grep across the surveyed web implementations returns no hits. MDN describes the `reduce` value as indicating "a user has enabled the setting on their device for reduced motion", intended for users for whom animation "can trigger discomfort for those with vestibular motion disorders". A word swapping in place every 75 ms is not panning or scaling, so whether RSVP is a vestibular trigger is **UNVERIFIED and I found no study addressing it** — but the media query is a two-line CSS check with no dependency, and the correct response is obvious: start paused rather than autoplaying, and default to a lower WPM. This is the cheapest unclaimed win in the entire survey.
- **The mechanism actively removes an accessibility affordance.** Benedetto's framing, citing [Schotter, Tran & Rayner 2014](https://www.tsw.it/wp-content/uploads/Rapid-serial-visual-presentation-in-reading-The-case-of-Spritz-1.pdf): regressions are "backward gaze moves within a line, produced to re-examine material not clearly perceived or understood", they constitute 10–15% of reading time, and "the functional removal of regressions jeopardizes comprehension". RSVP does not merely fail to help re-reading; it makes re-reading impossible without a rewind control. This is the strongest available argument that **rewind is an accessibility feature, not a convenience feature** — which reframes jetzt's semantic rewind (§6 item 5) from a nice affordance into the load-bearing one.
- **The dyslexia claim is marketing, and I could not source it.** RSVP tools are frequently pitched as helping dyslexic readers; Benedetto's introduction notes only that "visually impaired, dyslexic and less efficient readers have also been proposed to benefit from RSVP displays" citing work from 1984–1986. I found **no modern controlled study** supporting a dyslexia benefit for ORP-style RSVP. Marked UNVERIFIED — do not repeat the claim.

### Timing model bugs

- **jetzt gives 14+ character words *less* time than 12-character words.** `switch(len)` has cases through 13 and no default, so `mul` stays 1 for any length ≥14. At 800 WPM a 20-char technical term gets 75 ms while "complicated" gets 135 ms.
- **Reedy has a dead band:** `+0.3` when `textLength < 4 || > 7`, then a further `+0.4` only when `10 < textLength < 14`. A 14-char token scores 0.4 *lower* than a 12-char one.
- **Sprint Reader's SUBTLEX-US model is calibrated on movie subtitles.** Unknown words pin to the 300 ms (200 WPM) floor, so on an academic paper essentially every technical term hits the ceiling — and in that mode the WPM setting does nothing at all.
- **Word-frequency weighting may simply not be worth it.** The ReadKinetic author, who implemented both: *"I tried syllable estimates and word frequency weighting too, but neither was noticeably better than punctuation plus length, and they made the rhythm less predictable, which turned out to matter more than being clever."* This is one developer's subjective judgement, not a controlled study — but it is the only head-to-head report from someone who built both, and it cuts against Kairo's ~120-coefficient model and against [Thoth](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.01699), whose entire Results section contains no WPM figures, no comprehension scores, no participants and no baseline.

### The premise itself

[Benedetto et al. 2015](https://www.tsw.it/wp-content/uploads/Rapid-serial-visual-presentation-in-reading-The-case-of-Spritz-1.pdf), *Computers in Human Behavior* 45:352–358, DOI 10.1016/j.chb.2014.12.043. Conditions: N=60 (30 female, mean age 28, SD 9), native French speakers, naive to Spritz, between-subjects, first chapter of a French Orwell translation (6169 words), nominal 250 wpm → actual ~225, self-paced pauses permitted, ~25-minute sessions, SMI RED 5 eye tracker at 500 Hz, 22" Dell P2210 at 1280×768, 70 cm viewing distance. Three participants excluded from comprehension analysis, eight from eye-tracking (<85% tracking ratio). Full text retrieved and every figure below checked against the paper's Table 1 on 2026-08-29.

**Read this caveat before quoting the numbers: they did not test Spritz.** In §2.3 the authors state that "the software employed for simulating the RSVP procedure was developed according to the information provided by Maurer et al. (2014). This choice was dictated by the unavailability of an official SDK at the time we built the experiment." The stimulus was a Matlab R2012a + Psychtoolbox-3 reimplementation built from Spritz's *patent application* (US 20140186867) and a company blog post, not the shipped product. So this is a test of the published Spritz method — the same ORP table and length/sentence pause model reproduced in §3 above — and not of Spritz's server-side tokenizer, its duration normalization, or its actual timing constants. It is the right evidence for "does the technique work", and the wrong evidence for "was Spritz's implementation good".

Two further conditions that change how the speed result reads: pausing **was** permitted (spacebar), and pause time was added into the reading-time denominator, so Spritz's measured 209 wpm is its nominal 225 wpm minus time participants spent stopped — the deficit is participants choosing to halt, which is itself the finding. The authors note the converse would have been worse: "reading for more than 25 min without allowing the reader to naturally stop would have further worsened comprehension, visual fatigue, and task load, but also advantaged reading speed."

The [authors' summary](https://www.tsw.it/wp-content/uploads/Rapid-serial-visual-presentation-in-reading-The-case-of-Spritz-1.pdf): *"reading with Spritz impaired literal comprehension, increased visual fatigue and was more demanding with respect to traditional reading."* On the vendor claim: *"The developers of Spritz claim that it can be learned in less than five minutes, but there is no scientific evidence regarding this statement."* Note the authors' own scope caveat — they suspect Spritz "might be more suitable for very short texts".

**Do not overstate the fatigue result.** The "increased visual fatigue" in that summary rests entirely on the objective blink-rate proxy. The *subjective* six-item visual fatigue scale rose in both conditions and showed **no difference between them** (p=.106); the authors attribute the null to between-subjects designs being insensitive to this measure. Both groups reported roughly the same eye strain; only the instrument disagreed. Honest framing: RSVP measurably suppresses blinking, which is a known dry-eye mechanism, but participants did not *report* feeling worse.

Calibrate against [Brysbaert 2019](https://api.osf.io/v2/preprints/xynwg/), *J. Mem. Lang.* 109:104047 (190 studies, 18,573 participants): adult silent reading of English **non-fiction averages 238 wpm** (fiction 260; oral 183 from 77 studies / 5,965 participants), with most adults in 175–300 wpm for non-fiction. Benedetto's 200 wpm baseline is squarely normal. So the honest framing is: RSVP at ~210 wpm bought nothing and cost 12 comprehension points.

### Conflicts

**The comprehension literature does not agree, and the disagreement is about speed.**
- [Benedetto et al. 2015](https://www.tsw.it/wp-content/uploads/Rapid-serial-visual-presentation-in-reading-The-case-of-Spritz-1.pdf) found a 12-point literal deficit at ~209 wpm — i.e. at a *normal* rate.
- [Hester, Werner, Greenwald & Gunning 2016](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177/1541931213601300), "Exploring the Effects of Text Length and Difficulty on RSVP Reading", *Proc. HFES* 60(1):1294–1298. **Citation downgraded during audit.** I verified the bibliographic record via Crossref (title, authors, volume, issue, pages, year all confirmed) and the abstract's framing — *"Recent marketing efforts claim that by removing saccades, well designed Rapid Serial Visual Presentation (RSVP) text displays can increase reading speed without an overall loss of comprehension"* — but the specific finding previously quoted here as "no differences between comprehension for RSVP and traditional text displays at normal reading speeds" **is not present in any full text I could retrieve**. The Crossref abstract indicates the study is about text length and difficulty on small/smartwatch displays. Treat the null-result claim as UNVERIFIED; it is paywalled at SAGE and I could not reach the full text.
- [Acklin & Papesh 2017](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:29461715&resultType=core&format=json), "Modern Speed-Reading Apps Do Not Foster Reading Comprehension", *Am. J. Psychol.* 130(2):183–199, DOI 10.5406/amerjpsyc.130.2.0183. Full abstract retrieved and verified verbatim: tested static vs 700 vs 1000 wpm at 6th- and 12th-grade text difficulty; *"Static text was associated with superior performance, relative to either RSVP reading condition. Furthermore, slower RSVP speeds yielded better verbatim comprehension, and faster speeds benefited inferential comprehension"*; and *"participants' WMC did not predict comprehension scores."*

I trust [Benedetto](https://www.tsw.it/wp-content/uploads/Rapid-serial-visual-presentation-in-reading-The-case-of-Spritz-1.pdf) most for our purposes, but **not for the reason previously given here.** An earlier draft of this dossier claimed it "tested the actual Spritz ORP implementation"; that is false, and the paper says so plainly — it tested a Matlab reimplementation of the patent (see the caveat above). The defensible reasons to weight it are narrower: it is the only one of the three with eye tracking, the only one reporting full descriptives with effect sizes and a correlation matrix, and the only one whose stimulus was a long continuous text rather than short passages.

What is genuinely unresolved is the *shape* of the effect, not its existence. Acklin & Papesh's verbatim/inferential dissociation matches Benedetto's own pattern — literal comprehension down 12 points, inferential intact (44% vs 48%, n.s.) — across two labs, two languages, two speed regimes and two independent implementations. That convergence is the strongest signal in the literature and it points one way: **RSVP costs you the details and leaves the gist.** Acklin & Papesh's finding that *faster* RSVP helped inferential scores is the genuinely counterintuitive result and nobody has replicated it. Anyone quoting a single comprehension number from this literature is overclaiming; the honest summary is directional, not numeric.

**jetzt's citation stripper: best-in-class and still too blunt.** One researcher called it "the best academic in-text citation stripper of any RSVP project" (correct — [parse.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ds300/jetzt/master/modules/parse.js) requires a full author-year parenthetical with a 1600–2099 year, versus [speeedy's](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sami-29/speeedy/main/src/utils/text-utils.ts) `\[\s*\d[\d,;\s–-]*\]` which eats interval notation like `[0, 1]`). Another demonstrated that it deletes any parenthesised four-digit year and any bracketed integer: `'The effect was large (2019).'` → `'The effect was large.'`; `'n was 1024 [3] and 55 [17].'` → `'n was 1024 and 55.'`. Both are true. It is the right idea with the wrong blast radius, and it is off by default (`strip_citation: false`), so essentially nobody has exercised it.

**jetzt's dormancy date.** Reported as both "dormant since 2021" and "master frozen 2017-01-25". The [GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/ds300/jetzt) supports the latter; the 2021 figure is likely a repo-level `pushed_at` on a non-master ref. Treat 2017-01-25 as the last change to shipping code.

## 8. Unresolved / unverified

- **Real PDF extraction quality.** Every PDF path in this dossier was read, none was run against an actual paper. There is no measured digit-level error rate, column-interleave rate, or per-page timing for any of them. *Settled by:* running pdf.js `getTextContent`, PyMuPDF `get_text('text')`, and speeedy's line reconstruction over one 20-paper corpus with hand-checked ground truth focused on digits.
- **Whether Chrome still permits load-unpacked MV2 in developer mode.** The [official timeline](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/migrate/mv2-deprecation-timeline) says all MV2 extensions are disabled from Chrome 138 with no re-enable, but I confirmed by direct fetch that **it does not address unpacked/dev-mode installs at all**. UNVERIFIED. *Settled by:* one load-unpacked test on current Chrome, or the Chromium kill-switch source. Only affects how dead jetzt's extension path is; the bookmarklet works either way.
- **Whether Chrome's built-in PDF viewer structurally blocks content-script text-layer access.** Inferred from Reedy having no PDF code despite advertising PDF support, and from Sprint Reader user reports. No primary Chrome/Chromium document found stating it. UNVERIFIED. *Settled by:* an unpacked MV3 extension matching `file:///*` logging `getSelection()` and `body.innerText` from every frame on a local PDF.
- **Actual setTimeout drift at RSVP rates on target hardware.** No implementation publishes jitter distributions; the only citable magnitude is web.dev's qualitative "tens of milliseconds or more". *Settled by:* a harness logging `performance.now()` deltas across 5000 chained 75 ms timeouts. Run this before designing any compensator.
- **The frame-quantization arithmetic in §4** (66.7/83.3 ms alternation at 800 WPM on 60 Hz) is derived from MDN's statement that rAF matches refresh rate; the alternation has not been confirmed against actual paint timestamps. UNVERIFIED. *Settled by:* logging rAF timestamps around word swaps on a 60 Hz and a 120 Hz panel.
- **Whether Kairo's ~120-coefficient model beats speedread's five.** No A/B exists for any pair of RSVP timing models. *Settled by:* a within-subject comparison at fixed WPM with a comprehension quiz.
- **Whether Sprint Reader's SUBTLEX-US model is usable on academic text.** The prediction that every technical term pins to the 300 ms floor is inference, not measurement. *Settled by:* computing `-log2(p)` over a real paper's tokens against `wordfrequency-en-US.js` and plotting the durations against the 40–300 ms band.
- **Spritz's server-side tokenizer is unrecoverable.** ORP indices, multipliers, sentence splitting and >13-char hyphenation all lived on the dead API; the patent's Fig. 5a/5b tables are the only surviving evidence, and the 2019 SDK may have diverged from them. *Settled by:* a Wayback capture of an `api.spritzinc.com` payload, or a decompiled Spritz Android APK.
- **Spritz patent enforceability.** Google Patents shows US8903174B2 and US10332313B2 Active to 2033, but those are computed estimates and no post-2019 maintenance-fee or assignment check was done. Enforcement posture is real: jetzt's maintainer [wrote in 2014](https://api.github.com/repos/ds300/jetzt/issues/138) that he "got a semi-threatening email saying that we need to use this or I will face legal action", and the patent granted seven months later. *Settled by:* USPTO Patent Center lookup plus counsel. Not settleable by more searching.
- **Reedy's shipping v3.1.2 behaviour.** Every Reedy claim here describes the frozen MV2 repo, not the closed MV3 store build 40,000 people use. *Settled by:* downloading the CRX and diffing Parser/Sequencer.
- **Readsy's historical feature set.** `readsy.co` now 301-redirects to a casino domain; the Wayback capture was unfetchable. Every specific Readsy claim is UNVERIFIED.
- **E-ink perceptual severity.** KOReader's `FULL_REFRESH_COUNT = 6` and the per-word widget rebuild are in source, but no hardware measurement of latency or ghosting was taken. The only quantification is the ReadKinetic author's "about a word per second", which is one developer's recollection, not an instrument reading. *Settled by:* filming a Kobo/Kindle running FastReader at 200/400 WPM, or instrumenting `Screen:refreshPartial` timings.

*Added during the 2026-08-29 audit:*

- **Hester et al. 2016's actual finding.** This dossier previously quoted it as reporting no comprehension difference at normal reading speeds. The Crossref record confirms the paper exists exactly as cited (title, authors, HFES 60(1):1294–1298) but I could not retrieve the full text — it is paywalled at SAGE — and the quoted sentence appears nowhere I could reach. The abstract suggests a study about text length/difficulty on small displays, which may not be the "normal speeds" comparison claimed. **UNVERIFIED.** This matters because Hester is the dossier's only counterweight to Benedetto; if it does not say what was claimed, the literature is less conflicted than §7 presents and the case against RSVP is *stronger*, not weaker. *Settled by:* institutional access to the HFES proceedings, or emailing the authors.
- **Chrome's current intensive-throttling grace period.** Documented as 5 minutes (Chrome 88 blog, and `grace_period_seconds` in current `blink/common/features.cc`); proposed as 10 seconds for Chrome 109; API owners on the blink-dev thread asked for 1 minute and the thread as served is truncated before resolution. The shipped default is also field-trial configurable, so there may be no single answer. **UNVERIFIED.** *Settled by:* `chrome://version` plus a timing harness in a hidden tab, or `chrome://flags`/Finch config inspection. Low stakes — pausing on `visibilitychange` is correct at every candidate value.
- **Whether RSVP is a `prefers-reduced-motion` case at all.** A word replacing another in place is not panning or scaling, which are the documented vestibular triggers. No study found either way. **UNVERIFIED.** *Settled by:* it probably is not settleable cheaply; honour the query anyway, since it costs two lines and the failure mode of ignoring it is worse than the failure mode of respecting it.
- **The dyslexia / visually-impaired benefit claim.** Frequently asserted in RSVP marketing. Benedetto's introduction attributes it to sources from 1984–1986; I found no modern controlled study. **UNVERIFIED — do not repeat it.** *Settled by:* a literature search specifically on RSVP and developmental dyslexia post-2000.
- **Whether shipped Spritz outperformed its own patent.** Since Benedetto tested a Matlab reimplementation of the patent application, the possibility remains that the real SDK — with server-side tokenization and duration normalization — performed materially better than the 209 wpm / 60% result. Nobody ever ran the controlled study against the shipping product, and now nobody can: the API is dead. **Permanently UNVERIFIABLE.** This is the single biggest hole in the case against RSVP, and it cuts both ways — it also means no one ever demonstrated the product worked.

**Substantive errors found and fixed (6).**

1. **The dossier's stated reason for trusting its keystone source was false.** §7 Conflicts claimed Benedetto "tested the actual Spritz ORP implementation". The paper says the opposite in §2.3: the stimulus was a Matlab R2012a + Psychtoolbox-3 *simulation* built from the patent application because no SDK was available. The numbers are unaffected; the interpretation is not. Rewritten, with a prominent caveat before the results table, and the bottom-line summary amended. This was the most serious defect in the file — an uncited confident assertion propping up the document's headline conclusion.
2. **"10 seconds, default-on in Chrome 109" is not supported by its own citation.** The linked thread shows API owners pushing back and asking for 1 minute; the thread is truncated before resolution. Chromium source still shows a 5-minute default. Replaced with the verified four-condition mechanism, an explicit UNVERIFIED on the current value, and the observation that the recommendation is unchanged at any threshold — plus two genuinely useful facts recovered from the same sources (the `visibilitychange`-handler exemption, and the audio-silence condition).
3. **Chrome 88 intensive throttling was described by 1 of its 4 conditions**, making the trap sound automatic when it is not. Fixed.
4. **One-sided fatigue reporting.** The dossier reported the blink-rate deficit but omitted that the *subjective* visual-fatigue scale showed no difference between modalities (p=.106). Added to the table and the prose. This is a negative result inside the negative result and the file was quietly dropping it.
5. **Garbled methodology.** "Their design forbade pausing beyond 25 minutes" — pausing was in fact permitted throughout via spacebar, and pause time was folded into the reading-speed denominator, which is *why* 209 undershoots the 225 nominal. Corrected; the mechanism is more interesting than the error.
6. **A quotation had drifted from its source.** The e-ink ghosting quote was rendered as "reading through the residue of the last two or three words"; the actual text is "it comes with ghosting". Replaced with verified wording, drift flagged inline, and the unsourced "two or three words" figure dropped.

**Section filled (coverage test).** Required sections 5 and 7 were substantive; the gap was thematic rather than structural. **Accessibility was absent from all eight sections** despite RSVP being, definitionally, auto-updating text — a Level A WCAG 2.2 concern. Added a subsection to §7 covering SC 2.2.2 exposure, the universal absence of `prefers-reduced-motion` handling, the argument that removing regressions makes **rewind an accessibility feature rather than a convenience**, and an explicit UNVERIFIED marker on the widely repeated dyslexia-benefit claim. Cross-referenced from the bottom line as item 7a.

**Other content added.** Provenance block for the ReadKinetic thread (single-developer self-report, weight accordingly) since the dossier cites it repeatedly; verbatim upgrade of the software4scholars evidence; five new §8 entries.

**What I did not change.** The source-code sections (§2, §3, §4 rendering/ORP tables, §6, §7 numeric-fidelity table) are the strongest part of this document and every claim I sampled from them verified exactly. I left them alone. The §4 frame-quantization arithmetic remains flagged as derived-not-measured, which is the correct treatment.

**Top 3 genuinely unresolved after this pass.**

1. **Whether the comprehension literature actually conflicts.** With Hester downgraded to UNVERIFIED, the dossier's "the literature disagrees" framing now rests on one paper I could not read. If Hester does not say what was claimed, then Benedetto and Acklin & Papesh agree — RSVP costs verbatim detail — and there is no conflict to hedge against. Resolving this changes the project's premise, not a detail of it. Paywalled at SAGE; needs institutional access.
2. **Nobody has ever measured RSVP on the content this project targets.** Every comprehension study used novels or grade-levelled prose. Every result about numeric fidelity, columns, tables and equations in §7 is source-reading, not measurement — no digit-level error rate exists for any PDF path here. Since numeric corruption is identified as the disqualifying failure class *and* its signature property is that the output looks plausible, this is the gap most likely to sink the build, and it is cheap to close: one 20-paper corpus with hand-checked digits.
3. **Whether the technique is worth implementing well at all.** The strongest replicated finding is that RSVP loses literal detail while preserving gist. That is a coherent product — a skimming tool — but it is close to the opposite of what an academic-paper reader needs, and no source in this dossier resolves the tension. Acklin & Papesh's unreplicated finding that *faster* RSVP improved inferential comprehension is the one result that would change the design if it held.
