# Local VLM Extraction (Tier 2): Model, Serving, and Hallucination Dossier

*Compiled 2026-08-29 from four parallel research passes plus follow-up verification, then adversarially audited and corrected the same day (see **Audit note** at the end for what was wrong and what was verified). Substantive claims carry a source link; claims that do not are explicitly marked UNVERIFIED or ENGINEERING JUDGEMENT. Numbers are reported with their conditions.*

## Bottom line

1. **Run PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 (0.96B, Apache-2.0) as the primary tier-2 model, with LightOnOCR-2-1B (Apache-2.0) and olmOCR-2-7B-1025-FP8 (Apache-2.0) as the two escalation candidates to A/B against it.** PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 is the current OmniDocBench v1.6 leader at [96.33 per its model card / 96.34 per the leaderboard](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opendatalab/OmniDocBench/main/README.md), weighs [1.92 GB in bf16](https://huggingface.co/api/models/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR-VL-1.6?blobs=true), and is a first-class vLLM architecture ([`PaddleOCRVLForConditionalGeneration`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vllm-project/vllm/main/docs/models/supported_models.md)). **This pick is contested by one independent third-party harness** — the French PDF-to-Markdown benchmark ranks PaddleOCR-VL-1.5 at 0.381 all-categories, *below* olmOCR-2-7B-FP8 (0.461), LightOnOCR-2-1B (0.542) and dots.ocr (0.547) ([Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11960v1)). See §2 Conflicts. Treat the primary pick as a hypothesis to falsify locally, not a settled choice.
2. **VRAM is not your constraint; image tokens are.** olmOCR-2-FP8 is [~9.4 GiB of weights](https://huggingface.co/allenai/olmOCR-2-7B-1025-FP8), leaving ~20 GiB for KV. A 300 DPI A4 page is [~11,100 Qwen2.5-VL tokens](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) vs ~1,500 at olmOCR's trained [1288 px longest edge](https://huggingface.co/allenai/olmOCR-2-7B-1025-FP8) — a 7.4× swing in both cost and batch size.
3. **Use FP8, never INT4.** FP8 is free on OCR quality ([82.4±1.1 vs 82.3±1.1](https://huggingface.co/allenai/olmOCR-2-7B-1025-FP8)); INT4 W4A16 preserves glyph reading ([DocVQA 99.85% recovery](https://huggingface.co/RedHatAI/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct-quantized.w4a16)) but degrades number handling disproportionately ([MathVista 96.69%, MGSM 92.04%](https://huggingface.co/RedHatAI/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct-quantized.w4a16)).
4. **vLLM on Linux (WSL2 or container) is the only serious serving option on sm_120.** SGLang's own [SM120 optimization plan](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/issues/19637) still lists core kernels as unfinished. llama.cpp's `llama-server` has a [fine-grained OCR regression from build b8545, closed as not planned](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/22785) (single reporter, licence-plate recognition rather than document OCR — see §4).
5. **Expect roughly one page in ten to be materially wrong.** The best published content-faithfulness score is [90.0%](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08538v3) (Dots OCR 1.5, ParseBench, 2,078 human-verified enterprise pages) — note this is a **rule pass rate over 169,011 rules, not a page pass rate**, so "one page in ten" is an *inference* from it, not a measurement. Independently, a top-of-leaderboard model (Chandra, 83.1) coexists with a user-reported [~8–10% of documents showing token loops](https://github.com/datalab-to/chandra/issues/51) in production.
6. **Silent numeral substitution is the failure that matters and no built-in guard catches it.** olmOCR's four validity checks all pass on a fluent, EOS-terminated page with a flipped sign ([issue #41](https://github.com/allenai/olmocr/issues/41), opened 27 Feb 2025 — **now closed, with no fix or explanation recorded on the thread**, which is weaker evidence of an unsolved bug than an open issue but does not show it was fixed).
7. **Cross-checking tier 2 against tier 1 works — but only with order-invariant metrics.** Digit-multiset comparison is [shipped and validated](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08538v3); naive whole-page edit distance is [explicitly rejected](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11960v1) by two independent benchmark teams.
8. **No published source anywhere gives a per-digit accuracy rate on academic tables.** That measurement has to be built, not cited.

### Conflicts

- **Datalab's two model licences carry different dollar thresholds.** Surya OCR 2's LICENSE names **$5,000,000** prior-year gross revenue or total equity/debt funding; Chandra's LICENSE — same "AI PUBS OPEN RAIL-M LICENSE (MODIFIED), Version 0.1, 2 March 2023" header — names **$2,000,000**. Both add the same non-compete: you may not use the weights if you "provide or otherwise make available any product or service that competes with any product or service offered by" the licensor. Both impose share-alike on derivatives *and on the Output*. For a shipped extraction product the non-compete is the binding clause, not the threshold. Datalab's actual commercial pricing is **UNVERIFIED** — `datalab.to/pricing` renders client-side and returns only the string "Datalab" to a fetcher, and `docs.datalab.to` has an expired TLS certificate.
- **dots.ocr's "1.7B" marketing figure describes only the decoder.** The [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02498v4) specifies a 1.2B vision encoder trained from scratch plus a 1.7B Qwen2.5-1.5B-based decoder, ~2.9B total; OmniDocBench lists it as 3B.

**Excluded outright: GOT-OCR2.0.** Paper v1 from Sept 2024 with no revisions, repo last released [1 Feb 2025](https://github.com/Ucas-HaoranWei/GOT-OCR2.0), absent from the [current OmniDocBench v1.6 leaderboard](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opendatalab/OmniDocBench/main/README.md) which lists 32 systems including 0.1B models, multi-page mode explicitly "not batch inference", and pinned to a documented `cuda11.8+torch2.0.1` stack. sm_120 needs CUDA 12.8+; whether a Blackwell build exists at all is **UNVERIFIED**, but nothing recommends finding out.

### Conflicts

- **The two benchmarks invert the olmOCR-vs-MinerU ranking.** olmOCR-Bench: olmOCR 82.4 vs MinerU 75.2. OmniDocBench v1.6: MinerU2.5-Pro 95.75 vs olmOCR 85.74. Each lab's model wins its own harness, the metrics are incompatible (binary unit tests vs edit distance/TEDS/CDM), and OmniDocBench's olmOCR row carries no version string — it may be v1 (68.2 on olmOCR-Bench) rather than v2. **Trust neither ranking over the other.**
- **Vendor self-reports do not reconcile.** dots.ocr's paper claims [79.1±1.0](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02498v4) on olmOCR-Bench; MinerU's paper evaluates dots.ocr at [73.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.22186v2) — a 5.5-point spread on the same benchmark. Ai2's own two publications disagree on MinerU by 0.6 points ([75.2 in the bench README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/allenai/olmocr/main/olmocr/bench/README.md) vs [75.8 in the olmOCR 2 blog](https://allenai.org/blog/olmocr-2)). **Treat cross-vendor deltas under ~5 points as noise.**
- **A third, independent harness ranks the primary pick near the bottom.** The [French PDF-to-Markdown benchmark](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11960v1) (Feb 2026, unit-test style, French corpus) publishes an all-categories pass rate that neither vendor controls.   Three things here cut against this dossier's own recommendation and against the OmniDocBench ranking. (1) **PaddleOCR-VL-1.5 places 0.381, below olmOCR-2-FP8's 0.461** — the reverse of both OmniDocBench and the ArXiv column of olmOCR-Bench. (2) **PaddleOCR-VL-1.5 collapses on `long_table` (0.125)**, the category closest to "dense numeric academic table", which is this project's core case. (3) **PaddleOCR-VL-1.5 is 3.7× slower per page than olmOCR-2-FP8** (4.056 s vs 1.107 s) in this harness, destroying the "0.96B model must be fast" assumption that the parameter count implies. Caveats that limit how much weight to put on this: the corpus is **French**, the tested checkpoint is **1.5, not 1.6**, and both PaddleOCR and MinerU are pipeline systems whose full-page path may not have been driven through their official CLI. **This is the most important unreconciled conflict in the dossier and it is not resolvable from published sources — only by running the local harness.**

Trust the repository.

**What these benchmarks do not measure.** olmOCR-Bench's Tables category is 1,020 assertions over 188 PDFs — about 5.4 cell checks per table page, so a wrong digit in an untested cell is invisible. OmniDocBench uses TEDS and edit distance, where one flipped sign in a long table is a rounding error. An [independent critique](https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/olmocr-bench-review-insights-and-pitfalls-on-an-ocr-benchmark) (vendor blog, labelled as such) adds that olmOCR-Bench is ~56% academic content — which makes it *more* representative for this project than most — but that its unit tests were generated with Sonnet 3.7 and Gemini 2.0 Flash, discarding anything Gemini could not parse, biasing toward models derived from those systems.

**KV cache per token**, derived from published `config.json` files (arithmetic, not measured): 2 (K+V) × layers × kv_heads × head_dim × bytes.

- [Qwen2.5-VL-7B / olmOCR-2](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct/raw/main/config.json): 28 layers × 4 KV heads × 128 = **56 KiB/token** bf16.
- [Qwen3-VL-8B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct-FP8): 36 layers × 8 KV heads × 128 = **144 KiB/token** — 2.6× worse. Its native `max_position_embeddings` of 262,144 would need 36 GiB of KV alone; `--max-model-len` must be set explicitly.
- [Qwen2.5-VL-32B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-32B-Instruct/raw/main/config.json): 64 layers × 8 × 128 = **256 KiB/token**.
- [PaddleOCR-VL-1.6](https://huggingface.co/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR-VL-1.6/raw/main/config.json): 18 layers × 2 KV heads × **128 head_dim** = **18 KiB/token** bf16 — roughly **3.1× cheaper** than olmOCR-2 per token. (An earlier draft of this dossier used head_dim 64 and reported 9 KiB / "6× cheaper"; the config's explicit `head_dim` is 128, so that figure was wrong by 2×. Note `hidden_size` is 1024 while 16 heads × 128 = 2048, so the head dimension cannot be inferred from `hidden_size / num_heads` here — read `head_dim` directly.) Its `max_position_embeddings` is **131,072**, so the same explicit-`--max-model-len` discipline applies as for Qwen3-VL, though at 18 KiB/token a full 131k context is only ~2.25 GiB.

**Image tokens are the real budget.** Qwen2.5-VL uses 14×14 patches with 2×2 spatial merge, so one visual token = [28×28 = 784 pixels](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/en/model_doc/qwen2_5_vl), capped at 16,384 tokens by `max_pixels=12,845,056`.

| Render | Pixels | Qwen2.5-VL tokens | KV at 56 KiB/tok |
|---|---|---|---|
| olmOCR-2 default, 1288 px longest edge | 1.17 Mpx | ~1,500 | ~82 MiB |
| [LightOnOCR-2](https://huggingface.co/lightonai/LightOnOCR-2-1B), 200 DPI / 1540 px | 1.68 Mpx | ~2,140 | ~117 MiB |
| 150 DPI A4 | 3.87 Mpx / 2 | ~2,774 | ~152 MiB |
| 300 DPI A4 | 8.70 Mpx | ~11,100 | **~607 MiB** |

With ~9.4 GiB of olmOCR-2-FP8 weights and ~20 GiB usable for KV, that is roughly 33 concurrent 300-DPI pages or 240+ at 1288 px, halved again with `--kv-cache-dtype fp8`. The [ViT is windowed](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13923v1) (window 112, only layers 7/15/23/31 full-attention), so encoder cost scales linearly with page area — the quadratic blowup lives in the LLM.

**This token table is Qwen2.5-VL-specific and does not transfer to the primary pick.** PaddleOCR-VL-1.6's vision tower is a SigLIP-style encoder with `image_size` 384, `patch_size` 14, `spatial_merge_size` 2 ([config.json](https://huggingface.co/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR-VL-1.6/raw/main/config.json)) — a different tiling scheme with a different token count per page, and no published `max_pixels`-equivalent was found on the card or in the config. **How many visual tokens PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 emits for a full A4 page at a given DPI is UNVERIFIED** and has to be measured by counting `prompt_tokens` on a single request. Until it is measured, the concurrency arithmetic above applies only to the Qwen2.5-VL-based models.

**vLLM has first-class entries for every candidate that matters**: `PaddleOCRVLForConditionalGeneration` (`PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR-VL`), `DotsOCRForCausalLM`, `DeepseekOCRForCausalLM`, `DeepseekOCR2ForCausalLM`, `GlmOcrForConditionalGeneration`, `LightOnOCRForConditionalGeneration`, `HunYuanVLForConditionalGeneration`, plus `Qwen2_5_VLForConditionalGeneration` which olmOCR-2 rides ([supported_models.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vllm-project/vllm/main/docs/models/supported_models.md)). Chandra and MinerU are **not** listed — Chandra ships [its own `chandra_vllm` launcher](https://huggingface.co/datalab-to/chandra/raw/main/README.md); MinerU ships its own [HTTP server and multi-GPU router](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opendatalab/MinerU/master/README.md). Note the registry names `PaddleOCR-VL`, not `-1.6`; same architecture class, but confirm before pinning.

**Compatibility risks, in order of likelihood:**

1. **CUDA 13 breaks the install.** [Issue #37714](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/37714): five sequential failures on SM120/CUDA 13 — `libcudart.so.12` missing, FlashInfer JIT needing undocumented gcc/nvcc/ninja, Mamba block-size assertions, invalid FLASH_ATTN+fp8-KV combination. **Pin CUDA 12.8/12.9 wheels.**
2. **FlashInfer + NVFP4 + FP8-KV crashes on sm_120 — but only in a configuration this project should not be in.** [Issue #54225](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/54225) (open, 28 Aug 2026): RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Max-Q (sm_120, 96 GB), Qwen3.6-35B-A3B **NVFP4** with **fp8 KV**, vLLM 0.27.1 — "an illegal memory access was encountered", crashing after 63 requests, after 74 requests, and once on a 16-token first request; `--attention-backend TRITON_ATTN` ran 413 requests over 1h45m clean. [Issue #54331](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/54331) (open, 29 Aug 2026): same card class, CUDA-graph segfault in `cuGraphLaunch` after ~7–8 min sustained load, clean on 0.24.0 and present from 0.26.0; `enforce_eager=True` fixes it at **+23.6% time/round (495.6 s vs 401.1 s)**. **Both are scoped to NVFP4-quantized hybrid-GDN / MoE checkpoints; neither reporter tested a dense Qwen2.5-VL- or PaddleOCR-VL-class model, and #54331 explicitly reports a non-GDN model (Gemma4-26B) unaffected.** The transferable lesson is narrow: on sm_120 prefer dense FP8 over NVFP4, and keep `TRITON_ATTN` and `enforce_eager` in the back pocket as one-flag fallbacks. Do not treat these as evidence that a dense OCR VLM will crash.
3. **NVFP4 and FP8-MoE silently fall back.** [#47749](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/47749) falls back to Marlin W4A16; [#43507](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/43507) — CUTLASS ships no SM_120/121 specialization for FP8 grouped GEMM, so MoE drops to Triton. Dense FP8 is the safe lane.
4. **Multimodal memory profiling reserves for a worst-case 16,384-token image** and can OOM at startup even when real requests fit ([#20123](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/20123)). Set `--limit-mm-per-prompt '{"image":1,"video":0}'`, `--mm-processor-cache-gb 0`, an explicit `--max-model-len`, and clamp `max_pixels` via `mm_processor_kwargs`.
5. **Serving flags are correctness settings, not performance settings.** The [DeepSeek-OCR recipe](https://recipes.vllm.ai/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-OCR) says to "use the custom logits processor along with the model for optimal OCR and markdown generation performance" and ships `--mm-processor-cache-gb 0` / `enable_prefix_caching=False` in every command, justified as "OCR tasks do not typically benefit from prefix caching or image reuse". (An earlier draft said DeepSeek's recipe "mandates" the logits processor; the recipe's own word is "optimal", so that was an overstatement.) The [Unlimited-OCR recipe](https://recipes.vllm.ai/baidu/Unlimited-OCR) is the blunt one: "The custom logits processor is mandatory—omitting it causes documents to loop on coordinate tokens." The [LightOnOCR-2-1B card](https://huggingface.co/lightonai/LightOnOCR-2-1B/raw/main/README.md) independently prescribes the same three flags in its serving snippet: `--limit-mm-per-prompt '{"image": 1}' --mm-processor-cache-gb 0 --no-enable-prefix-caching`. Three unrelated vendors converging on the same flags is the strongest signal in this section.

6. **Grammar-constrained decoding is available and is not discussed anywhere else in this dossier.** vLLM's OpenAI-compatible server supports structured outputs by default via `xgrammar` or `guidance` backends, offering `choice`, `regex`, `json`, `grammar` (EBNF) and `structural_tag` ([docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/structured_outputs.html)). This bears directly on the failure taxonomy: a grammar that closes an HTML `<table>` cannot emit the unterminated-pipe loop reported in [Qwen3-VL#1611](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-VL/issues/1611), and a `regex`-constrained numeric field cannot emit `84.89` where `84-89` belongs. **What no retrieved source establishes: whether constraining an OCR VLM reduces or *increases* content error.** Constrained decoding forces the model off its natural distribution when the page does not fit the grammar, which is a plausible mechanism for turning a visible format failure into a silent content failure — the exact trade this dossier warns about elsewhere. The vLLM docs quoted above cover none of that; they document no performance cost and no multimodal interaction either way. **Treat structured outputs as an untested lever: worth an A/B, not worth shipping unmeasured — ENGINEERING JUDGEMENT, UNVERIFIED.**

**AWQ is a trap.** vLLM maintainers state ["AWQ quantization in vLLM only supports float16 (not bfloat16) due to kernel limitations"](https://discuss.vllm.ai/t/performance-degradation-and-compatibility-issues-with-awq-quantization-in-vllm-qwen2-5-vl-32b/1159) and that AWQ kernels "may be slower than non-quantized models on some hardware" — a reported 3–5× slowdown on Qwen2.5-VL-32B-AWQ (RTX A6000, Ampere; whether this reproduces on Blackwell is **UNVERIFIED**). A [working 5090 config](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-35B-A3B-GPTQ-Int4/discussions/3) used `--quantization gptq_marlin --dtype bfloat16 --kv-cache-dtype fp8`, ~31.3 GB VRAM at `--gpu-memory-utilization 0.9`, 194–197 tok/s sustained on WSL2/Ubuntu 24.04.

### Conflicts

**Does FP8 actually work well on a 5090 under WSL2?** [Issue #37242](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/37242) (opened 17 Mar 2026, **closed as not planned, labelled stale, zero maintainer engagement**) reports FP8 W8A8 at 44.9 tok/s, "3× slower than AWQ", asserting that "the RTX 5090's native FP8 tensor cores are not exposed through `dxgkrnl` yet". The same issue is also the sole source for this dossier's WSL2 tax: "bare metal Linux comparison: ~180-200 tok/s (WSL2 is ~70% of native)". So **both the FP8 warning and the ~30% WSL2 penalty are one unreviewed comment by one person, now closed without triage — UNVERIFIED, and they should not be cited as two independent facts.** It directly contradicts the merged [SM120 FP8 CUTLASS kernel](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/17280), which was validated on 5070 Ti and 5090. It matters enormously because FP8 is the only quantization with published zero-accuracy-cost evidence on OCR. **Benchmark FP8 vs gptq_marlin on the actual box before committing** — and note that if the claim is true, the fix is a native-Linux boot or a container on a Linux host, not a different quantization.

## 5. What quantization costs in OCR accuracy

**FP8: free.** olmOCR-2's authors publish both checkpoints under the same pipeline (olmOCR v0.4.0, olmOCR-Bench): [Overall 82.3±1.1 bf16 vs 82.4±1.1 FP8](https://huggingface.co/allenai/olmOCR-2-7B-1025-FP8); ArXiv 82.9/83.0, Tables 84.3/84.9, Old Scans 48.3/47.7. Every delta is inside the confidence interval. The recipe is minimal — `FP8_DYNAMIC`, targets `[Linear]`, ignoring `lm_head` and `model.visual.*` — so the entire vision tower stays bf16. [RedHatAI's FP8-Dynamic Qwen2.5-VL-7B](https://huggingface.co/RedHatAI/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct-FP8-Dynamic) is at or above baseline everywhere: DocVQA 94.27→94.27, ChartQA 86.44→86.80, MathVista 69.47→71.07.

**INT4: cheap on glyphs, expensive on numbers.** [W4A16 recovery vs bf16](https://huggingface.co/RedHatAI/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct-quantized.w4a16), GPTQ group_size 128, vision tower unquantized:

| Task | bf16 | W4A16 | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| DocVQA val | 94.27 | 94.13 | **99.85%** |
| ChartQA test CoT | 86.44 | 85.64 | 99.07% |
| VQAv2 val | 75.59 | 73.90 | 97.76% |
| MMMU val CoT | 52.00 | 51.11 | 98.29% |
| MathVista testmini CoT | 69.47 | 67.17 | **96.69%** |
| MGSM CoT | 56.38 | 51.89 | **92.04%** |

Read the pattern: transcribing glyphs survives INT4 almost intact; carrying a *number* through the language model is where the 3–8% goes. That is precisely the operation "re-emit this table cell-by-cell" performs.

**Negative result: there is no academic study of quantization's effect on document-OCR or numeral accuracy.** [arXiv:2601.15287](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.15287v1) tests BLIP-2 and LLaVA-1.5 at 2/4/6/8 bits but only on captioning, retrieval and VQA — no OCR, no FP8. The closest adjacent evidence is [arXiv:2502.01205](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01205v1), where fp16 beats 4-bit Q4_0 on OCR post-correction CER by 2.5–4.7 pp across four LLMs — but that is text-only post-correction, not a VLM reading a page. **Nobody has published "CER on numeric table cells vs bit width."**

**Resolution cuts against the model defaults.** [arXiv:2503.23667, *Context-Independent OCR with Multimodal LLMs: Effects of Image Resolution and Visual Complexity*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.23667) (submitted 31 Mar 2025 — **~17 months old, and it predates every model in this dossier; whether it still holds for 2026 checkpoints is UNVERIFIED**), a controlled **single-character** study, finds multimodal LLMs match conventional OCR "at about 300 ppi, yet their performance deteriorates significantly below 150 ppi" — context-independent by construction, i.e. the regime a digit in a table cell lives in. olmOCR's 1288 px render is ~110–117 ppi for a letter page. The reconciliation is that language priors carry running prose at low DPI and cannot carry isolated numerals. **UNVERIFIED whether rendering above the trained resolution helps or hurts olmOCR-2 — it is off-distribution as well as ~7× more expensive.**

## 6. Hallucination: measured rates and failure taxonomy

**Ceiling on rule-level correctness.** [ParseBench: A Document Parsing Benchmark for AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08538v3) (submitted 9 Apr 2026, rev. 13 Apr 2026; **2,078 human-verified annotated pages and 169,011 rules** — an earlier draft of this dossier said 507 pages / 147,319 rules, which is wrong) defines content faithfulness verbatim as measuring "whether parsed output preserves *all and only* the content present in the source document: no dropped text, no hallucinated text, and correct reading order", scored `CFS_d = (w_text·S_text + w_order·S_order)/Σ_C w_C` with `w_text = 1.0`, `w_order = 0.5`. Content-faithfulness scores (Table 5), full list:

| System | CFS | System | CFS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dots OCR 1.5 | **90.0** | Azure Doc Intelligence | 84.9 |
| LlamaParse Agentic | 89.68 | Extend | 84.1 |
| LandingAI | 88.6 | Google Cloud Doc AI | 83.7 |
| LlamaParse Cost Effective | 88.02 | GPT-5 Mini | 82.3 |
| Qwen 3 VL | 87.6 | Haiku 4.5 | 78.7 |
| Reducto | 86.4 | AWS Textract | 74.8 |
| Gemini 3 Flash | 86.2 | Docling | 66.9 |

Three conditions that limit this number. (1) **It is a rule pass rate, not a page pass rate** — 90.0% does not mean 90% of pages were clean, and no page-level figure was retrieved. (2) The corpus is enterprise insurance/finance/government, so dense math and two-column academic layouts are under-represented. (3) The paper's headline is a different, all-dimensions figure: "LlamaParse Agentic achieves the highest overall score at 84.9%" across five dimensions (tables, charts, content faithfulness, semantic formatting, visual grounding) — do not mix the two. **None of the eight models this dossier recommends between were evaluated by ParseBench**, so it bounds the field, not the candidates.

**Architecture matters more than scale.** [PP-OCRv6's hallucination benchmark](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13108) (rate of correct outputs with no invented text): PP-OCRv6_medium **93.20**, small 88.20, tiny 86.80, vs Kimi-K2.6 85.00, Qwen3-VL-235B 80.56, GPT-5.5 78.00, MiniMax-M3 72.60. A 34.5M-parameter CTC+NRTR recognizer beats a 235B VLM by 12.6 points on not-inventing-text, attributed to decoding "grounded in visual features" vs autoregressive language priors. **Vendor paper, top three rows are its own models — directionally trusted, absolute numbers not independently replicated.**

### Failure taxonomy

| Class | Evidence | Detectable by built-ins? |
|---|---|---|
| **Numeral substitution** | olmOCR-2 turned page-footer `61.6`→`74.6`, all-row `72.4`→`77.4`, list-item `81.2`→`81.6`, and hyphenated ranges `84-89`→`84.89`, `83-91`→`83.91` on one dense table ([CodeCut](https://codecut.ai/olmocr2-vs-paddleocr-vl/)). **Conditions: n=1 (a single 9-page PDF, the Docling technical report), olmOCR-2 as Q8_0 GGUF under llama.cpp b9380 on an Apple M5 Pro — so quantization and the llama.cpp server path are both confounds, and §4 records a separate `llama-server` digit-OCR regression. On the same page PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 (v3.6.0, CPU PaddlePaddle) preserved every value, which is a datapoint *for* the primary pick — from the same n=1 test.** Sign/subscript corruption in Planck asymmetric uncertainties ([olmocr#41](https://github.com/allenai/olmocr/issues/41), opened 27 Feb 2025 on arXiv 1807.06209 p.40, **now closed**, no recorded fix) | **No.** Fluent, EOS-terminated, in-budget output passes every check |
| **Silent omission** | "Completely misses Table 5" while detecting Table 4, in the same #41 report. Self-hosted olmOCR-2-FP8 (v0.4.25, Docker/EC2, separate vLLM container) logged `No document text for /input/table.pdf` with "Completed pages: 1, Failed pages: 0, Finished output tokens: 0", while the public demo parsed the same file ([#443](https://github.com/allenai/olmocr/issues/443), open since 18 Mar 2026, no maintainer response). dots.ocr shows "incomplete page transcription" as a systematic failure mode, attributed to a training bias against visual elements causing it to skip text-dense regions on colourful layouts ([French benchmark](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11960v1)) — **an earlier draft of this dossier attributed "missing paragraphs, 18/62" to that audit; that count could not be found in the paper and has been withdrawn. [Nougat](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.13418) measured 1.5% of pages. [DeepSeek-OCR#151](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-OCR/issues/151) (open since Oct 2025, unassigned): **9.2%** catastrophic failure cohort, failures defined as CER ≥ 0.9 (545/600 = 90.8% pass), on **1800s–1900s British Library newspaper clippings**, H100 80GB / CUDA 12.2 / bfloat16, `max_new_tokens` ~3072. | **Reading order** | Zero-shot Qwen2.5-VL-3B: **42%** of 50 audited historical-document predictions had major reading-order errors, vs 16% fine-tuned ([CHURRO](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19768v1)). | **Fabrication on degraded regions** | GPT-4o achieves **30.21%** hallucination-free accuracy on deliberately blurred/occluded text where the correct response is refusal; a purpose-trained 7B reaches 58.05% ([KIE-HVQA](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20168v1)) | **No** — models do not abstain |
| **Out-of-distribution confident substitution** | [GlotOCR](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12978v1): **68.4%** average cross-script hallucination rate on 148 low-resource scripts; models default to Latin/Arabic/Devanagari rather than failing. olmOCR hallucinated on blank pages until [v0.3.0](https://github.com/allenai/olmocr) because it was never trained on them | **No** |

**Decoding configuration is part of the hallucination surface.** olmOCR 2 states plainly that ["sampling at a lower temperature tends to give better results but at the risk of repetition loops"](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19817v1), fixed by escalating 0.1→0.8 on EOS failure; that switching JSON→YAML "reduced the retry rate dramatically"; and that v1 ran a **12% retry rate** in production. Note the interaction: temperature escalation trades a *detectable* failure for a higher-temperature re-read, which is a worse setting for numeric fidelity. A page needing 5 retries was read at temperature 0.8.

**Sampling penalties do not fix this.** The DeepSeek-OCR reporter applied `no_repeat_ngram_size` 5–7, `repetition_penalty` 1.15–1.25, `early_stopping`, column splitting and stricter-parameter retries: ["despite these measures, failure cohort remained unchanged at 9.2%"](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-OCR/issues/151). The Qwen reporter rules the penalty out on principle: ["Increasing the repetition penalty is not an acceptable solution because it breaks the transcription of naturally repetitive text, for example in tables."](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-VL/issues/1611)

**Leaderboard rank does not bound tail failure.** Chandra scores 83.1 — top of olmOCR-Bench — while a production user reports, verbatim, "Model often hallucinates with token loop sections. Model often returns empty responses": across ~60 **documents** (not pages), "5-6 of them on average always have token loop hallucination" (≈8–10%) and "1 or 2 documents consistently have missing pages" (≈2–3%) ([chandra#51](https://github.com/datalab-to/chandra/issues/51)). **Conditions: Russian and Kazakh scans described by the reporter as "decent quality" with "clearly recognizable text"; the rates are per document, not per page, so the per-page rate is lower and unknown; the claim that the repo's own vLLM settings were used is not stated in the thread and has been withdrawn from this dossier.** Open, unassigned, no maintainer response. The out-of-distribution-language confound is real — this may be a Russian/Kazakh result rather than a Chandra result — but it is still the sharpest available demonstration that a top-of-leaderboard score does not bound tail failure. The [French benchmark table in §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11960v1) corroborates the direction from the other side: Chandra tops that independent harness too (0.664 all-categories), so its leaderboard standing is not spurious, and it *still* produced this report in production.

**Old scans are a hard floor for every model.** olmOCR-Bench Old Scans column: Mistral 29.3, Marker 33.5, MinerU 33.7, DeepSeek-OCR 33.3, PaddleOCR-VL 37.8, Nanonets 40.9, olmOCR 47.7, Infinity-Parser 47.9, Chandra 50.4. Nobody exceeds 50.4. **This is a corpus problem, not a model-selection problem** — and it is aggravated in a two-tier design, because tier 2 disproportionately receives exactly the pages tier 1 failed on.

## 7. Detection methods: evidenced vs plausible

### Evidenced and cheap

| Method | Evidence | Catches |
|---|---|---|
| `finish_reason != 'stop'` | [olmocr/pipeline.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/allenai/olmocr/main/olmocr/pipeline.py) treats non-EOS as a failed page | Repetition loops |
| Trailing repeated n-gram >30 + non-target-script characters | olmOCR-Bench's Baseline class ("no repeated n-gram >30, no CJK/emoji in English output", per the [dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/olmOCR-bench/raw/main/README.md)). The [French benchmark's](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11960v1) analogous `baseline` column spreads models from **MinerU2.5-1.2B at 0.795** and granite-docling-258M at 0.877 up to **0.988–1.000 for every 1B+ VLM tested**, i.e. the check does separate degenerate systems — **but that paper glosses `baseline` as "simple, well-formatted text that serves as a sanity check", not explicitly as a repetition/script guard, so whether it is the same class as olmOCR-Bench's is UNVERIFIED.** An earlier draft of this dossier asserted the repetition reading; it is now flagged as contested | Degeneration, language switch |
| Token-count ceiling | `total_tokens > MODEL_MAX_CONTEXT` (16384) in olmocr pipeline | Runaway generation |
| **Digit-multiset comparison** | [ParseBench's `bag_of_digit_percent`](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08538v3), shipped, "compare[s] digit frequency distributions to catch common OCR errors (e.g., 6 → 8)" | Numeral substitution; **order-invariant**, so it survives dehyphenation and column reordering |
| **Pairwise precedence assertions** | ParseBench: "passes if and only if the first occurrence of `before` precedes the last occurrence of `after`" | Reading-order breaks, without penalizing legitimate reformatting |
| Max-logit variance (window B=15, threshold 6.75) | [Nougat](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.13418), with early stop at 3.375 over the last 200 tokens | Repetition specifically. **Thresholds will not transfer numerically to a decoder-only VLM — UNVERIFIED for Qwen-class models** |

### Evidenced but weak

- **Consensus Entropy** ([arXiv:2504.11101](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11101v4)) — pairwise normalized edit-distance agreement across several VLMs, then entropy. Error-detection **F1 48.0–51.3** vs **36.1–40.0 for VLM-as-Judge** (a 42.1% relative improvement), measured on **1,000 real-world PDF pages hand-scored 1.0 (perfect) → 0.0 (mostly incorrect) on text-matching accuracy**. Ensembling (Ovis2-1B + Qwen2.5-VL-7B + Step1V + Step1o) reached **OCRBench 955 vs the 926 prior SOTA, +29**. Routing on OCRBench-V2 with GPT-4o: **θ=0.5 → +3.8 accuracy with 15% of samples routed; θ=0.8 → +6.8 with 48.7% routed.** *(An earlier draft of this dossier reported "θ=0.5 escalated 7.3% of inputs for +8.2%"; those figures could not be reproduced from the v4 HTML and have been replaced with the retrieved ones. The corrected numbers are worse for the technique: at a 15% escalation budget you buy under 4 points.)* **That F1 is the best automatic bad-page detector retrieved anywhere, and it is barely a coin flip — and it costs an N-model ensemble, which one 32 GB card cannot host.**
- **Latent-representation probes** ([arXiv:2511.19806](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.19806v1)) — average abstention accuracy **0.750** (range 0.709–0.789) on Qwen2.5-VL-7B/Gemma3-12B hidden states. Requires hidden-state access, so unusable behind a plain OpenAI-compatible endpoint. No AUROC reported.

### Measured failures — do not build on these

- **Token logprobs.** Average token log-probability reaches only **66.0–67.0%** abstention accuracy; the paper states outright that ["output logits in VLMs are often severely miscalibrated."](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.19806v1) First-token probing is [9.4±1.3 AUROC points worse](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.11741v1) than 10-token aggregation.
- **Whole-page edit distance.** [The French benchmark team](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11960v1): "global edit-distance metrics on the whole page become less relevant once transcription is mostly correct," because differences are dominated by formatting choice. LlamaIndex report a semantically correct table scoring [TEDS 0.63 / edit distance 0.56](https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/omnidocbench-is-saturated-what-s-next-for-ocr-benchmarks) purely for emitting HTML instead of LaTeX — a ~44% penalty for zero semantic error.
- **Semantic-embedding similarity and word-voting.** [Consensus Entropy](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11101v4) reports VL-Uncertainty (semantic clustering on LLM embeddings) achieving only "modest gains on structured OCR (+3.3%)" while degrading on semantic tasks, and ROVER-style word-level voting failing catastrophically off closed-vocabulary ASR: **Doc-VQA −42.1%, Math-VQA −92.0%**. *(An earlier draft cited "+0.2%" for VL-Uncertainty and "−33.8%" for ROVER along with a quote about character differences being invisible to embeddings; none of those three could be reproduced from the v4 HTML and all have been replaced by the retrieved figures. The conclusion is unchanged and the ROVER result is far more damning than the number it replaces.)*
- **Same-model repeated decoding.** T=0.7 × 3 runs of identical models yields only [+2.1% average](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11101v4). On one 32 GB card you can hold one 7B OCR VLM; running it twice buys almost nothing. **The affordable diversity is a different *kind* of extractor — tier 1, or a small CTC head — not a second decode.**
- **Prompting for abstention.** Prompt-to-abstain, verbalized confidence, VLM-as-judge and R-Tuning were all beaten by a trained probe in the same table. There is no evidence that "say UNREADABLE if you can't read it" produces reliable abstention.

### Does cross-checking against tier 1 work, and by what metric?

**No published study measures a tier-1 (deterministic) vs tier-2 (VLM) cross-pipeline diff on academic PDFs.** Everything found compares VLM-to-VLM or VLM-to-gold. The honest answer is assembled from adjacent evidence:

- **It works if and only if the metric is order- and normalization-invariant.** Tier-1/tier-2 differences on academic PDFs are dominated by *legitimate* transformations: dehyphenation, column reordering (tier 2 often fixes what the PDF content stream gets wrong), header/footer removal, ligature normalization, math re-encoding. Character-level NED cannot separate these from hallucination — that is the explicit finding above.
- **The recommended primary metric is the digit multiset delta**, after stripping page numbers and superscript reference markers. It is the only cross-check with a shipping, published precedent, and it is invariant to every legitimate transformation listed. Its documented blind spot, stated by ParseBench itself: "frequency-based comparison cannot detect swaps between equally frequent digits (e.g., if every 6 becomes 8 and vice versa, the distribution is unchanged)." It also cannot localize which number is wrong.
- **Secondary metrics — ENGINEERING REASONING, not evidenced:** rare-token (hapax) recall from tier 1 into tier 2 (reordering preserves token identity; omission and substitution do not); `len(tier2)/len(tier1)` outside an empirically-set band, which catches truncation and looping in one number; sentence-level LCS coverage. **No source reports AUROC or precision/recall for any of these on document OCR — UNVERIFIED.**
- **Document anchoring (feeding tier-1 text into the prompt) is a different thing and is largely deprecated.** Ai2's own ablation: [74.7 without vs 75.5 with](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18443v2), against a ±1.1 interval — inside the noise band — and *negative* on Tables (−0.4), Old Scans (−0.6), Old Scans Math (−0.2). olmOCR 2's shipped inference prompt is [`build_no_anchoring_v4_yaml_prompt()`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/allenai/olmocr/main/olmocr/pipeline.py), image-only, with no PDF-derived text. **Feeding anchors to olmOCR-2 is off-distribution input to a narrowly fine-tuned model — the exact condition that produced the blank-page hallucination bug. Do not do it without a local A/B.**
- **Two structural cautions.** First, tier 1 is itself frequently wrong (broken ligatures, wrong Unicode for math, scrambled column order), so a detector that flags every disagreement will have a large false-positive rate driven by tier-1 defects — CHURRO found [4% of its own gold standard omitted parts of the page](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19768v1). Second, olmOCR's own design uses deterministic text as a *router and safety net*, not as prompt context: [`PdfFilter`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/allenai/olmocr/main/olmocr/filter/filter.py) gates entry (drops fillable forms, keeps documents with <200 extractable characters or alpha-ratio <0.50), and `make_fallback_result()` falls back to pdftotext after retries. **That is the shape to copy.**

## 8. Recommendation

**Primary: PaddleOCR-VL-1.6, FP8 or bf16, served by vLLM under WSL2/Linux, pages rendered at ~1300–1600 px longest edge.**

Rationale: Apache-2.0 with no thresholds and no non-compete; [958.6M params / 1.92 GB](https://huggingface.co/api/models/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR-VL-1.6?blobs=true) leaves the entire card for KV and concurrency at **~18 KiB/token, ~3.1× cheaper** than olmOCR-2; leads OmniDocBench v1.6 and posts the highest ArXiv category score (85.7) on the Ai2 leaderboard; a first-class vLLM architecture class; and — uniquely — it comes from the lab whose sibling recognizer [tops the only published hallucination benchmark](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13108).

**The case against it, stated plainly, because this dossier exists to surface it:** both benchmarks it leads are benchmarks its own lab or a partner lab publishes on, and the one fully independent harness retrieved ranks its immediate predecessor **7th of 12 systems, worst-but-one on long tables, and slowest but two per page** ([§2 Conflicts](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11960v1)). The 96.33/96.34 OmniDocBench score is also a text-edit + TEDS + CDM composite that, by the harness's own formula, **excludes reading order from the headline** and cannot see a single flipped digit in a long table. **Two of the three strongest arguments for this model are therefore measured by metrics blind to this project's actual failure mode.** Ship it as the default because it is small, permissive and serviceable; treat the first local harness run as a real go/no-go, not a formality.

**Runner-up (a): LightOnOCR-2-1B.** Apache-2.0, 1B, self-reported [83.2 on olmOCR-Bench](https://huggingface.co/lightonai/LightOnOCR-2-1B) — above olmOCR-2's independently-measured 82.4 — 0.542 all-categories on the independent French harness (above olmOCR-2's 0.461), 1.207 s/page there, 5.71 pages/s on an H100, a first-class vLLM class (`LightOnOCRForConditionalGeneration`), and a card that prescribes exactly the flags §4 recommends. Against it: the 83.2 comes off a rendered image on a vendor card, its throughput figure states no engine, concurrency or corpus, and it has no published FP8-vs-bf16 accuracy pair. **This model was absent from the previous draft of this dossier; on the evidence retrieved it is a stronger runner-up than olmOCR-2 on every axis except documentation quality.**

**Runner-up (b): olmOCR-2-7B-1025-FP8.** Apache-2.0, [82.4±1.1 overall](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/allenai/olmocr/main/README.md), the best-documented pipeline in the field (four reusable validity signals, a filter stage, a fallback stage), and the only checkpoint with a published FP8-vs-bf16 accuracy pair. Against it: a ~10-month-old checkpoint on a superseded Qwen2.5-VL base with no successor on the Hub as of Aug 2026 ([PyPI history](https://pypi.org/project/olmocr/#history) shows toolkit releases through v0.4.27 in Mar 2026 but no new weights), 9.4 GiB weights, and open unresolved issues for both silent omission and sign corruption.

**Conditions that flip the choice:**

- **PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 fails a local numeric-fidelity harness on two-column arXiv pages, *or* fails to reproduce its OmniDocBench standing on long numeric tables → switch.** The [French benchmark](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11960v1) already predicts this failure: PaddleOCR-VL-**1.5** scores 0.125 on `long_table` and 0.381 all-categories, behind olmOCR-2-FP8 (0.461) and LightOnOCR-2-1B (0.542). If the local harness reproduces that ordering on version 1.6, the OmniDocBench lead is harness-specific and the pick is wrong. Note also PaddleOCR-VL's `transformers` path is [element-level only](https://huggingface.co/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR-VL-1.6/raw/main/README.md) — a full-page parse depends on the official CLI or the vLLM path, which is one more moving part than an end-to-end model needs.
- **PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 measures slower than ~1.5 pages/s on the box → switch to LightOnOCR-2-1B.** The independent `time/page` column puts PaddleOCR-VL-1.5 at 4.056 s/page against LightOnOCR-2-1B's 1.207 and olmOCR-2-FP8's 1.107. A 0.96B model that runs 3.7× slower than a 7B one has an implementation problem, not a capacity one, and on a single card throughput is the whole argument for the small model.
- **The corpus turns out to be dominated by pre-1990 scans → neither model is adequate**, and the right response is to flag those pages rather than escalate them: nobody exceeds 50.4 on olmOCR-Bench Old Scans.
- **FP8 measures slow under WSL2 (the `dxgkrnl` report) → use bf16**, which both models fit comfortably, rather than dropping to INT4.

**Reject on licence, not on quality — say so honestly:** Marker weights, Surya OCR 2 and Chandra. Chandra tops olmOCR-Bench (83.1) *and* the independent French harness (0.664, comfortably first among open models); Surya OCR 2 self-reports 83.3 and is the **only model in this dossier with a measured throughput figure on the exact target GPU**. Both are blocked by the same [AI Pubs Open RAIL-M (Modified) v0.1, 2 March 2023](https://huggingface.co/datalab-to/chandra/raw/main/LICENSE) terms: the non-compete ("for any purpose if You … provide[] or otherwise make[] available any product or service that competes with any product or service offered by or made available by Licensor") and the share-alike, which verbatim extends "to the Output and any derivatives, changes or improvements to or of the Output" — i.e. it reaches the extracted text itself, not just the weights. **If this project is not a competing product and can live with share-alike output, Chandra is the accuracy-optimal choice and this recommendation changes.** That is a legal question, not a technical one. Also reject: Qwen2.5-VL-32B (worse than the 7B on [DocVQA 94.8 vs 95.7 and CC-OCR 77.1 vs 77.8](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-32B-Instruct/raw/main/README.md) while costing 2× the VRAM); GOT-OCR2.0 (abandoned); dots.ocr (strictly dominated by MinerU — worse on every OmniDocBench sub-metric and ~7× slower per page — though it retains MIT and multilingual breadth).

**Mandatory guards regardless of model choice**, in order of value per line of code: (1) digit-multiset delta vs tier 1, page-level; (2) `finish_reason != 'stop'` plus trailing-repeated-n-gram >30 plus non-target-script check; (3) `len(tier2)/len(tier1)` band; (4) a blank/near-blank ink-density gate *upstream* of the VLM — never send a blank page to a model that was never trained on them; (5) `--no-enable-prefix-caching --mm-processor-cache-gb 0` and an n-gram logits processor where the model's recipe calls for one. And **override olmOCR's `max_page_error_rate=0.004`** if reusing its pipeline: a 250-page book with two bad pages exceeds that threshold and yields nothing, which is correct for corpus-building and exactly wrong for a reader.

## 9. Unresolved / unverified

- **No measured pages/sec for any OCR VLM on an RTX 5090 except Surya OCR 2** (5.35 pg/s, 128 concurrency). Everything else is bandwidth-ratio arithmetic ignoring prefill compute and the WSL2 tax. *Settled by:* running the target model over 100 real pages at both 1288 px and 300 DPI on the actual box, wall-clocked.
- **Whether FP8 tensor cores are genuinely unavailable on RTX 5090 under WSL2.** One vLLM issue thread claims a `dxgkrnl` limitation; no NVIDIA or Microsoft documentation corroborates it. *Settled by:* a tok/s comparison of an FP8-Dynamic checkpoint vs a gptq_marlin build on the box. **Highest-value single experiment in this dossier.**
- **Per-digit / per-cell numeric accuracy on academic tables, for any model.** olmOCR-Bench tests ~5.4 cell values per table PDF; OmniDocBench uses TEDS and edit distance. *Settled by:* extracting every number from ~200 pages via both tiers, diffing multisets, hand-adjudicating disagreements. Doubles as the production gate.
- **Whether the digit-multiset check's precision is usable in practice**, given that tier 1 itself is frequently wrong. *Settled by:* the same harness, measuring false-positive rate attributable to tier-1 defects separately from tier-2 hallucinations.
- **Which of two contradictory independent rankings of PaddleOCR-VL is right.** OmniDocBench v1.6 puts it first at 96.33/96.34; the [French PDF-to-Markdown benchmark](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11960v1) puts version 1.5 at 0.381 all-categories, 7th of 12, with `long_table` 0.125 and 4.056 s/page. Version skew (1.5 vs 1.6), corpus language (French), and whether the official page-level CLI path was used are all candidate explanations, and none can be checked from the published artefacts. **This is the largest single unresolved question in the dossier and it invalidates or confirms the primary recommendation.** *Settled by:* running 1.6 and olmOCR-2-FP8 and LightOnOCR-2-1B over the same 100 local pages with a digit-multiset and long-table gate.
- **PaddleOCR-VL-1.6's visual token count per page.** Its encoder is SigLIP-style (384², patch 14, `spatial_merge_size` 2), not Qwen2.5-VL's 28×28-pixel-per-token scheme, and no `max_pixels` equivalent was found. Every concurrency and KV number in §3 is therefore Qwen-specific. *Settled by:* one request, reading `usage.prompt_tokens`.
- **Whether grammar-constrained decoding helps or hurts.** vLLM ships `json`/`regex`/`grammar`/`structural_tag` on the OpenAI endpoint and a grammar can make a table-pipe loop unrepresentable, but no retrieved source measures constrained decoding's effect on OCR *content* error, and the mechanism by which it could convert a loud format failure into a silent content failure is obvious. *Settled by:* an A/B on numeric-heavy pages with the digit-multiset gate as the scorer.
- **Whether LightOnOCR-2-1B's 83.2 olmOCR-Bench score is real.** It sits above olmOCR-2's independently-verified 82.4 but is published as a rendered image on the vendor's own card, and Ai2 has not run it. *Settled by:* running olmOCR-Bench locally, or waiting for it to appear in Ai2's table.
- **How much of the CodeCut olmOCR-2 digit drift (61.6→74.6) is Q8_0 GGUF quantization vs the model.** n=1, on Metal, not the FP8/vLLM path. *Settled by:* running the same page at bf16, FP8 and Q8_0 and diffing digit multisets.
- **Whether feeding tier-1 text into olmOCR-2's prompt helps, hurts, or goes off-distribution.** The v1 ablation does not transfer — v1 was trained *with* anchor text, v2 was not. *Settled by:* a local A/B on ~100 numeric-heavy pages before it enters any design.
- **Which olmOCR version OmniDocBench's 85.74 row refers to.** The row reads only "olmOCR | Specialized VLMs | 7B". If v1 (68.2 on olmOCR-Bench), the olmOCR-vs-MinerU contradiction largely dissolves. *Settled by:* checking OmniDocBench's per-model config files.
- **Whether the vLLM registry's `PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR-VL` entry serves the `-1.6` checkpoint.** Same architecture class (`PaddleOCRVLForConditionalGeneration`) but a different repo id. *Settled by:* `vllm serve PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR-VL-1.6` on the pinned version.
- **Whether Nougat's max-logit-variance repetition detector transfers to decoder-only VLMs.** Mechanism is plausible and cheap to test since vLLM returns logprobs; the numeric thresholds certainly do not transfer. *Settled by:* logging top-logprob variance over a few hundred pages, checking for bimodality against the trailing-n-gram detector as ground truth.
- **Datalab's actual commercial licence price** for Marker/Surya/Chandra weights. *Settled by:* opening `datalab.to/pricing` in a real browser, or emailing Datalab. Only the $2M/$5M thresholds and the non-compete are verified, from the LICENSE files.
- **HalluText / OCRAssistor's reported +9.6% hallucination improvement** — OpenReview served a bot wall on both the forum page and the PDF, so those numbers come from snippets only. *Settled by:* fetching via a browser session or an arXiv mirror.
- **Whether olmOCR-2's Apache-2.0 weights carry residual restriction from GPT-4o/GPT-4.1-generated training labels.** Weights are Apache-2.0, datasets are [ODC-BY](https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/olmOCR-mix-1025/raw/main/README.md), but both cards add non-binding "intended for research and educational use" language. *Settled by:* a lawyer, not a search engine.
- **Per-model reading-order error rate on modern two-column LaTeX papers specifically.** Closest evidence is olmOCR-Bench's 884 multi-column tests (aggregate only) and CHURRO's 42%/16% on historical documents with a 3B model. Whether a 7B OCR VLM errs at 2% or 20% on an arXiv two-column page is not established by anything retrieved.

### Arithmetic error found

**PaddleOCR-VL-1.6's KV cache was wrong by 2×.** The dossier used head_dim 64 → 9 KiB/token → "~6× cheaper than olmOCR-2". Its `config.json` states `head_dim: 128` explicitly — and `hidden_size` is 1024 with 16 heads, so head_dim is *not* derivable as `hidden_size / num_heads` here, which is presumably how the error arose. Correct value: **18 KiB/token, ~3.1× cheaper.** Fixed in the bottom line, §3 and §8. Also newly noted: `max_position_embeddings` 131,072.
